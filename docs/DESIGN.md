# Kilit — Phase 1 Design Document (final, revision 5 (frozen) — after critique round 3 and the owner's seven decisions)

## 1. Overview, goals, non-goals

Kilit is a 1:1 end-to-end-encrypted iOS messenger (Swift/SwiftUI, iOS 17+, CryptoKit only) with a self-hosted Go 1.24 store-and-forward server (single static binary, `modernc.org/sqlite`, no cgo). Its distinguishing property is that content is *hard to capture on the recipient's device*: message and photo content renders inside a secure-canvas layer that iOS blanks in screenshots, recordings, AirPlay and the App Switcher (best effort); recording/mirroring is detected via `isSceneCaptured` and content is replaced while it is active; screenshots are detected after the fact, logged and reported to the peer through the encrypted channel; content is hidden until held; messages disappear on a per-conversation timer; every rendered frame carries a per-viewer forensic watermark (with an offline decoder shipped in phase 1); and an opt-in, default-off, photosensitivity-gated 120 Hz flicker mode degrades casual camera snapshots. The server never sees plaintext, timers, capture events or attachment keys. **What the server does see:** every envelope body is integrity-protected (AEAD over `CONCAT(AD, sid, header)`, §3.3), but its routing fields are plaintext to the server — `v`, `kind`, `sid`, the `x3dh` block (public identity/ephemeral keys, `spk_id`, `opk_id`) and `hdr` (ratchet public key, `pn`, `n`). Of these only `sid` and `hdr` are *authenticated* (they are inside the AAD); `v`, `kind` and the `x3dh` block are **visible and unauthenticated**. Tampering with these routing fields can make an envelope undecryptable (classified, §3.2b step 4), park it (§3.2b step 2), or — only when the sender is already an accepted contact, and at most once per peer per 10 minutes — trigger the rejected-initiation recovery of §3.2 step 3: one bundle fetch (which costs one of the peer's OPKs) and a fresh session whose first message is `ctrl.resync`. It can never produce plaintext or a forged message, and never alters what decrypts. The server therefore learns session-establishment events, per-direction message counters and DH-ratchet step timing in addition to who/when/sizes. Header encryption (Double Ratchet §4 header keys, envelope `v:2`) is a phase-2 item. The phase-1 goal is the shortest path to a demonstrable two-phone conversation with all eight features, keeping the amount of Swift that must compile first-time on the owner's Mac as small as possible: encrypted JSON documents instead of a SQLite C-API layer, single-shot attachments, REST-only sending, an `LAContext` gate instead of a Secure Enclave key wrap.

**Phase-1 scope statement for feature 5 (watermark):** the pattern is rendered on every protected frame *and* decodable with `server/cmd/wmdecode` (Go, offline, phase 1). The per-pixel delta map is integer-exact and identical in Go and Swift (§5.9); calibration of amplitude and cell size against real photos happens on the owner's devices using the checklist in §7; the two knobs are documented in §5.9.

**Localisation decision:** phase 1 ships a **Turkish source language** app: `developmentRegion = tr`, `knownRegions = (tr, en, Base)`, `Localizable.xcstrings` with `sourceLanguage = "tr"` and a complete `en` translation (§6 gives the JSON shape). Devices set to English get English; every other device language gets Turkish (the development language). Keys are identifiers (`badge.green`), looked up with `String(localized: "badge.green", defaultValue: "Karartma + algılama")` (iOS 15+), so a missing catalog entry shows the Turkish default rather than the key. The single source for all user-facing safety text is **§10 of this document**; `docs/COPY.md` (owned by WP0) is a verbatim copy of §10, and WP8 and the READMEs copy from it verbatim.

**Non-goals / honest limits (verbatim in onboarding card 3, README, `docs/COPY.md` and `docs/THREAT_MODEL.md`; Turkish text in §10.4):**
1. A second camera pointed at the screen cannot be prevented. The watermark gives attribution, the flicker is a deterrent, hold-to-reveal shrinks the exposed surface. Never say "cannot be recorded".
2. Screenshot/recording blanking relies on an undocumented UIKit rendering side effect of `isSecureTextEntry`; it uses only public calls, is feature-flagged, canary-checked, and may stop working on a future iOS. Detection after the fact is the guaranteed floor. Your own unsent draft in the composer is *not* blanked (deliberate trade-off, see §5.5); it is covered only by the App Switcher privacy cover.
3. `userDidTakeScreenshotNotification` posts after the PNG exists; the first frames after a recording starts may be captured before `isSceneCaptured` flips; macOS iPhone Mirroring (reported on iOS 26) bypasses both mechanisms.
4. A jailbroken or modified peer client can ignore timers, suppress capture reports and keep plaintext. UI wording is "no screenshot was *reported*", never "none was taken".
5. The server and network see metadata (who, when, sizes, session-establishment events and message counters).
6. Devices without a passcode have no meaningful at-rest protection; registration is refused on them.
7. Flicker cannot exceed the panel refresh rate; any exposure ≥ 1/60 s, a two-frame burst or video reconstructs the content; it is disabled on 60 Hz panels.
8. In a 1:1 chat either party can compute the other's watermark tag, so the mark is evidence, not proof.
9. VoiceOver / Speak Screen read protected content aloud and can be recorded with any microphone; an optional setting hides protected content from assistive technologies at the cost of accessibility.
10. No groups, audio, video, multi-device, account recovery (new phone = new identity = "safety number changed"), push content, or Notification Service Extension. APNs sending is a phase-2 hook; messages arrive when the app is foregrounded.

## 3. Cryptographic protocol

Primitives: X25519 (`Curve25519.KeyAgreement` / `crypto/ecdh`), pure Ed25519 (`Curve25519.Signing` / `crypto/ed25519`; the 32-byte seed is the transport form, Go uses `NewKeyFromSeed`), SHA-256, HKDF-SHA256 (`HKDF<SHA256>.deriveKey` / `crypto/hkdf.Key`), HMAC-SHA256, ChaCha20-Poly1305 (`ChaChaPoly` / `golang.org/x/crypto/chacha20poly1305`). `Encode(PK)` = raw 32 bytes. All integers big-endian. `zeros` = 32 zero bytes (equal to empty salt per RFC 5869; a vector proves it). Every DH output is checked for all-zero with a constant-time OR-accumulate in Swift and rejected (`KilitCryptoError.lowOrderPoint`), matching Go's `crypto/ecdh` error; every received X25519/Ed25519 public key must be exactly 32 bytes (`KilitCryptoError.badKeyLength`) before use. Usernames are canonicalised to lowercase ASCII `^[a-z0-9_]{3,32}$` before signing, AD construction or hashing. `device_id` is a lowercase UUIDv4 string (36 ASCII chars, regex `^[0-9a-f]{8}-[0-9a-f]{4}-4[0-9a-f]{3}-[89ab][0-9a-f]{3}-[0-9a-f]{12}$`), validated by server and client. `server_origin` is the lowercase canonical origin the client talks to: `scheme://host[:port]`, no path, no trailing slash, default ports (80/443) omitted; the server rebuilds it from its `-public-origin` flag. No RawSpan-era CryptoKit overloads (iOS 26 only). Ed25519 signatures are never compared byte-for-byte across platforms (CryptoKit's are randomized); they are verified.

**Ed25519 strictness (protocol rule, identical in Go and Swift, unconditional):** both `crypto/ed25519` and CryptoKit accept small-order `A`, so a pre-check runs in *both* `pkg/kilitcrypto/sign.go` and `Crypto/Identity.swift` before the library verify: (1) signature length exactly 64; (2) `S = sig[32:64]` interpreted little-endian must be `< L` (compared bytewise against the constant `L = 2^252 + 27742317777372353535851937790883648493`); (3) `A` (the public key) and `R = sig[0:32]` must not be one of the small-order encodings (table in `primitives.json`, key `ed25519_small_order_points`, with the `+2^255` sign-bit variants included — 14 encodings). **Exact bytes (so a Linux agent can emit the table without a reference implementation):** `L` as 32 little-endian bytes = `edd3f55c1a631258d69cf7a2def9de1400000000000000000000000000000010`; `S < L` is decided by comparing `sig[32:64]` against these bytes **from index 31 downwards** (most significant byte first; the first differing byte decides; equal → reject). The small-order table is the 7 entries of libsodium's `ed25519_blacklist` (`crypto_core/ed25519/ref10/ed25519_ref10.c`, function `ge25519_has_small_order`, which masks bit 255 before comparing) expanded to both sign-bit values: `0000…00` and `0000…80` (order 4), `0100…00` and `0100…80` (identity, order 1), `26e8958fc2b227b045c3f489f2ef98f0d5dfac05d3c63339b13802886d53fc05` and `…fc85` (order 8), `c7176a703d4dd84fba3c0b760d10670f2a2053fa2c39ccc64ec7fd7792ac037a` and `…03fa` (order 8), `ecff…ff7f` and `ecff…ffff` (p − 1, order 2), `edff…ff7f` and `edff…ffff` (p, non-canonical 0), `eeff…ff7f` and `eeff…ffff` (p + 1, non-canonical 1) — each `…` is a run of `ff` to 32 bytes. libsodium's table (and ed25519-dalek's `EIGHT_TORSION`) is the authority; WP1 must verify every entry against it before emitting the vector (the 8 decodable entries can also be verified by point multiplication: `8·P = identity`). Violations → `KilitCryptoError.badSignature` (a bad public key at import time → `KilitCryptoError.badPublicKey`). The negative vectors are generated from the Go wrapper, so the vector set is identical on both sides; the rule is recorded in `docs/PROTOCOL.md`.

JSON conventions (both sides, so logs and envelopes look alike even though no JSON is ever compared byte-for-byte): Swift `JSONEncoder` with `[.sortedKeys, .withoutEscapingSlashes]`; Go `json.Encoder` with `SetEscapeHTML(false)` and struct fields declared in alphabetical order. **Optional fields (`opk_id`, `att`, `ref`, `x3dh`, `opks_sig`, `created_at` where optional): receivers must treat an absent key and an explicit `null` identically; senders may emit either** (Swift's synthesized `Codable` omits, Go's `*T` emits `null`). `envelope.json` contains both spellings and the Go/Swift round-trip tests cover both. Base64url is the unpadded URL alphabet (RFC 4648 §5); Swift has no stock encoder, so `Bytes.swift` provides `base64url(_:)`/`Data(base64url:)` tested against `base64url.json` (inputs of length 0, 1, 2, 3 mod 3 and bytes ≥ 0x80). **Decoding is strict on both sides:** decoders reject padding characters (`=`), any character outside `A-Za-z0-9-_` (including whitespace, `+` and `/`), encoded lengths `≡ 1 (mod 4)`, and non-zero trailing bits in the last character (Go: `base64.RawURLEncoding.Strict()`; Swift: an explicit check in `Data(base64url:)`). A field that fails to decode is `bad_envelope`/`400 bad_json`, never silently accepted; this keeps `msg_id` and `sid` canonical for the dedupe keys. `base64url.json` carries a `negative` list with one case for each of the four rules.

### 3.1 Identity and prekeys

| Key | Type | Lifetime | Storage |
|---|---|---|---|
| `IK_sig` | Ed25519 seed (32 B) | permanent | Keychain `identity.ed25519.v1` |
| `IK_dh` | X25519 | permanent | Keychain `identity.x25519.v1` |
| `SPK` + `spk_id` (uint32, monotonic) + `created_at` | X25519 | rotate when the current SPK is older than 7 d (checked on launch and foreground, then every hour while running); **every SPK private key is deleted when `now − created_at > 28 d`, regardless of whether a rotation happened** (checked on launch and foreground; a live `RatchetState.DHs` owns its own copy, so SPK deletion never breaks a session); a `prekey` envelope referencing an `spk_id` no longer stored → `spk_gone` (§3.2b) | `index.json` |
| `OPK_i` + `opk_id` (uint32) | X25519 | one use; 100 at registration; refill to 100 when server reports < 20 or sends `prekeys_low`; **private key deleted only (a) on first successful use in X3DH, or (b) 7 days after the id first appeared in `consumed_ids`** — never on age, never on the report alone; `consumed_ids` is purely a refill signal; at most 500 stored (when a refill would exceed 500, the oldest by `uploadedAt` are deleted first, and those ids are never re-uploaded) | `index.json` |

> **Rationale (OPK deletion):** the server reports an id as consumed the instant a bundle fetch claims it, but the initiator's first message is sent later (unbounded gap: offline, backgrounded, attachment upload first). Deleting on the report alone would turn every such initiation into `opk_gone`; a malicious server could trigger it at will. With the grace window an `opk_gone` classification is impossible for an id inside it; `PrekeyLifecycleTests` (Swift) and `TestOPKClaimThenLateSend` (Go store) cover fetch → offline → send.

> **Reviewer note (r3, OPK age rule dropped).** Revision 2 also deleted an unconsumed OPK private key 30 days after upload. The server has no OPK expiry and no withdraw API, so after 30 days of low traffic every unconsumed OPK would still be served by `GET /v1/users/{u}/prekeys` and every honest initiation claiming one would be `opk_gone`. Rule (c) is removed (option (a) of the review); the 500-key cap bounds storage, and `PrekeyLifecycleTests` asserts that an unconsumed key is **not** deleted at 30, 90 or 365 days.

> **Reviewer note (r3, SPK retention vs `seen_ek`).** Replay protection must not depend on two unrelated timers. The invariant is: *an `ek` stays in `seen_ek` at least as long as any SPK private key that could serve it is stored.* SPK private keys live at most 28 d after `created_at` (above), `seen_ek` entries expire after 45 d and the `(author, id)` dedupe window is 45 d (§3.2), so a replayed SPK-only initiation can never find its SPK after its `ek` has left `seen_ek`. The `replay` scenario has a `replay_after_expiry` case (§3.8).

Signatures (all with `IK_sig`, `||` = concatenation, `0x00` = single separator byte, `nonce` = the 32-byte server challenge nonce, `ts` = client unix seconds corrected by the server offset learned from the challenge, `created_at` = unix seconds the SPK was generated):
- `ik_bind_sig = Sig("Kilit_IKBind_v1" || IK_dh_pub)`
- `spk.sig = Sig("Kilit_SPK_v1" || u32BE(spk_id) || u64BE(created_at) || SPK_pub)`
- `opks_sig = Sig("Kilit_OPKBatch_v1" || u32BE(count) || (u32BE(opk_id_1) || OPK_pub_1) || … || (u32BE(opk_id_count) || OPK_pub_count))` over the batch in request order; required whenever `opks` is non-empty (register and `PUT /v1/prekeys`)
- `register_proof = Sig("Kilit_Register_v1" || 0x00 || username || 0x00 || IK_dh_pub || 0x00 || device_id || 0x00 || nonce || 0x00 || u64BE(ts) || 0x00 || server_origin)`
- `login_sig = Sig("Kilit_Auth_v1" || 0x00 || username || 0x00 || nonce || 0x00 || u64BE(ts) || 0x00 || device_id || 0x00 || server_origin)`
- `delete_sig = Sig("Kilit_Delete_v1" || 0x00 || username || 0x00 || nonce || 0x00 || u64BE(ts) || 0x00 || server_origin)`

Published bundle: `{username, ik_sig, ik_dh, ik_bind_sig, spk:{id, pub, sig, created_at}, opk:{id,pub}|null}`. Server verifies `ik_bind_sig`, `spk.sig`, `opks_sig`, `register_proof` on upload; clients re-verify `ik_bind_sig` and `spk.sig` on bundle fetch, and `ik_bind_sig` in every `prekey` envelope (server untrusted); the responder trusts its own stored SPK by `spk_id` (the `x3dh` block carries only `spk_id`, not the SPK public key or its signature, so there is nothing to re-verify there). **SPK freshness:** an initiator rejects a bundle whose `spk.created_at` is older than 21 days (`KilitCryptoError.staleSPK`, UI "Sunucu eski bir anahtar sunuyor"); `GET /v1/users/{u}/prekeys` returns only the newest SPK row. Because the responder keeps every SPK private key for 28 days after `created_at`, any SPK an initiator accepts (≤ 21 d old) still has its private key on the responder for at least 7 more days; an initiation that arrives later than that is classified `spk_gone` and, for an accepted contact (at most once per peer per 10 min), recovered by the rejected-initiation path of §3.2 step 3 (for a pending message request it is classified only).

**Identity uniqueness and proof of possession:** `register_proof` proves possession of `IK_sig`; possession of `IK_dh` is implicit — an initiation to the account can only be read by the holder of `IK_dh`, and the AD binds both keys and both usernames (§3.2), so a registration with someone else's `IK_dh` yields an account that can never read a message. `users.identity_sign_pub` and `users.identity_dh_pub` are `UNIQUE` (`409 key_in_use`), which together with the AD username binding is the defence against identity misbinding.

### 3.2 X3DH session setup (Alice → Bob)

```
EK_A = fresh X25519
DH1 = X25519(IK_dh_A, SPK_B)   DH2 = X25519(EK_A, IK_dh_B)
DH3 = X25519(EK_A, SPK_B)      DH4 = X25519(EK_A, OPK_B)        # only if OPK present
SK  = HKDF(ikm = 0xFF*32 || DH1 || DH2 || DH3 [|| DH4], salt = zeros,
           info = "Kilit_X3DH_v1_X25519_SHA256_ChaChaPoly", L = 32)
WS  = HKDF(ikm = SK, salt = zeros, info = "Kilit_WM_Secret_v1", L = 32)   # watermark secret
SID = SHA256("Kilit_SID_v1" || EK_A_pub)[0:8]                            # session routing hint
AD  = IK_sig_A || IK_dh_A || IK_sig_B || IK_dh_B
      || u16BE(len(uA)) || uA || u16BE(len(uB)) || uB            # initiator first
```
Alice deletes the EK private key and the DH outputs, runs `RatchetInitAlice(SK, SPK_B)` and stores `{sid, ws, ad, identity keys of both parties, role = initiator, createdAt, pendingX3DH: {ek_pub, spk_id, opk_id?}}` with the session. While `pendingX3DH` is present it is copied into every outgoing envelope on this session as the `x3dh` block (`kind:"prekey"`, with the identity keys and `ik_bind_sig` taken from the local identity); it is cleared (set to `nil`) by the first successful decrypt of a peer message on this session, after which envelopes are `kind:"msg"`. So a lost or delayed first message never strands the session, and the second and later `prekey` envelopes are built from stored state, not from a retained `EK` private key.

**Responder rules (Bob), in order, for a `prekey` envelope from username `from`:**
1. *Identity binding (fixes the key-substitution bypass).* Verify `ik_bind_sig` and the 32-byte lengths; recompute `sid` from `ek` and ignore the carried `sid`. If a contact record for `from` exists, the envelope's `ik_sig`/`ik_dh` must be byte-equal to the record; otherwise this is a **key change**: no session is created, no OPK is consumed, the envelope is parked (reason `key_change`, §3.2b step 2), the blocking "Güvenlik numarası değişti" banner is shown, and X3DH runs only after the user taps "Yeni anahtarı kabul et" (which replaces the record, marks every session whose AD identity keys differ from the new record as *superseded* (`oldIdentity`), resets `wm_secrets`, and re-runs the inbound pipeline for the parked envelopes of that peer). If no record exists, Bob performs `GET /v1/users/{from}`: a `200` with byte-equal keys continues (trust-on-first-use); a `200` with different keys or a `404` is `KilitCryptoError.identityMismatch` (envelope classified undecryptable, nothing stored); **any transient failure (offline, 5xx, 429, timeout, `401 invalid_token` until re-login) parks the envelope with reason `lookup_deferred`** and retries at 10 s, 1 m, 10 m, 1 h, then hourly; after 24 h without a definitive answer it is classified `lookup_failed`. The Safety Number (§3.6) is defined over the identity keys in the *session AD*; code asserts they equal the contact record.
2. *Routing to an existing session, then replay exclusion.* **If any session with this peer — live or superseded — has `sid == sid(ek)`, the envelope is decrypted on that session, regardless of `seen_ek`** (the normal "Alice has not heard back yet" case; also a late or replayed first message delivered after its `seen_ek` entry has expired, which then simply fails as `decrypt_failed` with the state unchanged — the `replay_after_expiry` case of §3.8 asserts this at t > 45 d). A `prekey` envelope whose `sid(ek)` matches an existing session never re-runs X3DH and never reaches step 3. Only otherwise does `seen_ek` apply: `sessions/<peer>.json` keeps `seen_ek`, the last 64 accepted `ek` values (45-day expiry, which is longer than the 28-day SPK private-key lifetime of §3.1 by construction); if `ek ∈ seen_ek` (and, by the above, no session with `sid(ek)` exists) → reject as `replay`, nothing changes.
3. *Fresh initiation.* `spk_id` not stored → `spk_gone`; `opk_id` present but not stored → `opk_gone`. Mirror the DHs with `(SPK_B, IK_dh_A)`, `(IK_dh_B, EK_A)`, `(SPK_B, EK_A)`, `(OPK_B, EK_A)`, run `RatchetInitBob(SK, SPK_B keypair)` (the state owns a copy of the SPK private key), decrypt on a candidate state, and only on success commit the session (with `sid`, `ws`, `ad`, `role = responder`, `createdAt`), add `ek` to `seen_ek`, delete `OPK_B`'s private key, **apply supersession rule S1 below**, and re-run the pipeline for envelopes parked under this `sid`. Inner message dedupe key is **`(author, id)`** per conversation (last 10 000 entries / 45 days); a duplicate is acked and dropped silently. **Rejected initiation (recovery path):** the trigger set is **exactly `spk_gone` and `opk_gone`**. An AEAD failure on the candidate state is classified `decrypt_failed` and never triggers recovery: an honest fresh initiation never fails AEAD, so such a failure is a forgery or a replay and there is nothing to recover. The path fires **only if a contact record for `from` already exists and the conversation is accepted** (a pending message request, step 4, is classified only), and **never when `sid(ek)` equals the `sid` of an existing session (step 2 routes there first) or is already in `known_sids`**. When it fires, Bob records `sid(ek)` in `known_sids` with `deletedReason = rejected_initiation`, classifies the envelope as usual (§3.2b step 4), and **immediately fetches a bundle and initiates a new session whose first inner message is `ctrl.resync{lost_sid: sid(ek)}`** (§3.4), rate-limited to one such initiation per peer per 10 min (further rejections inside the window are classified only). Without this, Alice would keep the dead own-initiated session as her sending session (it has no `lastActivity`), encrypt every later message into the void and show `.sent`; honest triggers exist (rule (b) grace exceeded while Alice was offline, SPK older than 28 d at the responder, a server that serves an already-claimed OPK id). The same bound is what limits a server that rewrites the unauthenticated `spk_id`/`opk_id`: it can make Bob burn one of Alice's OPKs per 10 min, nothing more (§1, §2). The `rejected_initiation` scenario of `ratchet_conversation.json` asserts that Alice's sending `sid` switches and her messages are re-sent.
4. *Unknown sender (message request).* If `from` was not a contact, step 1 created the contact with `verified = false` and the conversation flagged "Bilinmeyen gönderen"; messages are stored but the first reveal is gated behind "Kabul et"; "Engelle" drops all future envelopes from that username locally (server-side block list is phase 2). A conversation counts as *accepted* (the precondition of the rejected-initiation recovery path in step 3) only after "Kabul et"; until then a rejected initiation from that sender is classified only — no bundle fetch, no OPK spent on a sender the user has not accepted.

**Multiple sessions per peer, supersession and the sending-session rule.** Each `Session` carries `sid`, `role`, `createdAt`, `lastActivity?` (timestamp of the last successful decrypt of a *peer* message on it; own sends do not count) and `superseded: Bool` (sticky, never cleared). Every envelope carries `sid`; the receiver selects the session by `sid` — there is no trial decryption across sessions. **Supersession triggers:**
- **S1 (peer moved on):** when a new *responder* session is committed from a peer `prekey` envelope (step 3), every other session with that peer that already has `lastActivity` becomes superseded. Sessions without a decrypt are untouched (this preserves the crossed case). A peer initiates a fresh session only when it has no usable session, so a fresh initiation is proof that it has lost the old one.
- **S2 (key change):** on accepted key change, every session whose AD identity keys differ from the new contact record becomes superseded (`oldIdentity`).
- **S3 (local loss):** a session whose state fails the rollback check (§3.7) or cannot be parsed is **deleted** (not superseded) and its `sid` stays in `known_sids` with `deletedReason = rollback` or `parse_failure`; the client immediately fetches a bundle and initiates a new session whose first inner message is `ctrl.resync{lost_sid}` (§3.4). **S3 trigger bound (the server can rewrite the plaintext `sid` of any envelope):** `known_sids` entries are `{sid, deletedReason, deletedAt}` with `deletedReason ∈ {rollback, parse_failure, rejected_initiation, pruned}`; an inbound `msg` whose `sid` is unknown but in `known_sids` triggers S3 **only** if `deletedReason ∈ {rollback, parse_failure}` and at most once per peer per 10 min; sids of sessions pruned by age or by the 4-per-peer cap (`pruned`) and sids added by the rejected-initiation path never trigger S3 — the envelope is parked as `unknown_sid` and dropped after 24 h. Because `sid` is inside the AEAD AAD (§3.3), a rewritten `sid` can never decrypt on another session; the cost of a forged envelope is bounded to one parked row.

Superseded sessions still decrypt (late messages), never send, never un-supersede, and are deleted 30 days after `lastActivity`; at most 4 sessions per peer (beyond that the oldest superseded one is deleted). **Sending-session rule (deterministic, clock-free, symmetric):** candidates = non-superseded sessions with this peer; if any candidate has `lastActivity`, the one with the lexicographically smallest `sid` is the sending session; otherwise the newest own-initiated candidate (by `createdAt`); if there is none, fetch a bundle and initiate. On a crossed initiation both sides converge after one round trip (each side first replies on the session it decrypted; neither crossed session had a decrypt when the other's initiation arrived, so S1 does not fire; the first message received on the other session gives both sessions a decrypt and the min-`sid` rule picks the same one on both devices). After a loss (S3) the other side's old session is superseded by S1 the moment the new initiation decrypts, so it stops sending into the void. SPK-only sessions are accepted and shown as "peer's one-time keys exhausted". `ratchet_conversation.json` scenarios `crossed`, `lost_session`, `reinit_after_rollback` and `key_change_then_send` assert the sending `sid` on both sides at every step.

> **Reviewer note (time-windowed tie-break).** The proposal to let a peer-initiated session become the sending session immediately and to apply the crossed tie-break only to initiations within 5 minutes is not adopted as written: a time window needs synchronised clocks and makes the vectors clock-dependent. Rule S1 gives the same outcome where it matters (an old session with a decrypt is superseded by a fresh initiation) and leaves the crossed case deterministic without a clock.

> **Reviewer note (replay fix, partial adoption).** Keeping a new responder session out of the sending role "until the peer's first DH-ratchet reply" is not adopted: in the normal case Bob's only session is a responder session on which only the initial message has decrypted, and Bob must reply on it. Replay is excluded structurally by `seen_ek`, dedupe prevents re-display, and the `lastActivity`/min-`sid` rule prevents a replay-created session from displacing a live one.

> **Reviewer note (trial-decryption order).** Superseded by the `sid` routing hint: selection is by `sid`, rollback-on-copy applies to the single selected session.

### 3.2b Inbound processing policy (identical in Go reference and Swift)

For every fetched envelope (WS live push or hello/poll drain; the client deduplicates on `msg_id` because both paths can deliver the same row):
0. **Outer dedupe before any KDF.** `conv/<peer>.json` keeps `processed_msg_ids` (outer `msg_id` of every envelope that reached step 3(a); last 10 000 / 30 days). A known `msg_id` → ack silently (`already_processed`, no system row; the ack goes through `pendingAcks[]` like every other, step 5). This is what makes crash redelivery deterministic: after a crash between 3(b) and 3(c) the ratchet has advanced and the inner JSON can never be recovered, so the outer id is the only usable key.
1. Parse outer JSON; `v != 1`, unknown `kind`, `sid` missing, not valid strict base64url, or decoding to a length other than 8 bytes (on the wire `sid` is 11 base64url characters), `hdr` not exactly 41 bytes after decoding (`KilitCryptoError.badHeader`), `hdr[0] != 0x01` (`badHeader`), `ct` shorter than 256 + 16 bytes (an empty padded block is impossible, §3.3) → **undecryptable** (`bad_envelope`) before any KDF. For `prekey` envelopes `sid` is recomputed from `ek` and the carried field is ignored. **Error layering:** the envelope parser rejects `len(hdr) != 41` with `badHeader`; a `dh_pub` that is not 32 bytes cannot occur in a 41-byte header, so `badKeyLength` is raised only by the ratchet API when it is called directly with a parsed `MessageHeader` whose `dhPub` is not 32 bytes (the Set B case in §3.8), never from the inbound pipeline.
2. Route by `sid`/`kind` as in §3.2 (a `prekey` envelope whose recomputed `sid(ek)` matches any existing session, live or superseded, is decrypted on that session before `seen_ek` is consulted, §3.2 step 2). **Parking (never ack while parked):** an envelope that cannot be processed *yet* is stored in `conv/<peer>.json` → `parked[]` as `{msg_id, kind, sid, envelope, receivedAt, reason}`; bound 100 per peer (oldest dropped and acked as `parked_overflow`). Reasons and lifetimes: `key_change` (7 d, until acceptance), `unknown_sid` (24 h, re-run whenever a session with that `sid` is committed), `lookup_deferred` (24 h, retry schedule in §3.2 step 1). A `msg` whose `sid` is unknown is parked; **additionally, if that `sid` is in `known_sids` (every `sid` this device ever had with the peer, kept 90 days) with `deletedReason ∈ {rollback, parse_failure}`** the device has lost that session (S3): it initiates a fresh session with `ctrl.resync{lost_sid}` at once, at most once per peer per 10 min (§3.2 S3 trigger bound; other `deletedReason` values only park). On expiry the envelope is classified undecryptable with its reason (`unknown_sid`, `lookup_failed`, `key_change_expired`) and acked. Then run `RatchetDecrypt` on a *copy* of the state.
3. On success, in this order: (a) append the plaintext message **and the outer `msg_id`** to `conv/<peer>.json` (atomic write); (b) commit the advanced ratchet state (and `seen_ek`) to `sessions/<peer>.json` (atomic write); (c) write `index.json` with the advanced `cursor` **and the `msg_id` appended to `pendingAcks[]`** (one atomic write, so a persisted cursor always implies a persisted pending ack; this is the same write every other ack reason uses, step 5); (d) send the ack from `pendingAcks[]` (WS `ack` frame or `POST /v1/messages/ack`), and remove the id from `pendingAcks[]` on the next `index.json` write after a `200 {deleted}` (REST) or, for the WS path, after the next REST ack round-trip confirms it (the WS ack is fire-and-forget, so `pendingAcks` is flushed through REST on launch, foreground and every 60 s while connected). Unsent entries are retried from the outbox with the 5 s–10 min backoff; `pendingAcks` is bounded to 5 000 entries (oldest dropped). **The server never redelivers a row with `id ≤ after`** (§4.4 drain and `GET /v1/messages` both page `id > after`; a WS test asserts it), so a lost ack is recovered only by this persisted list, never by redelivery. A crash between (a) and (b) leads to redelivery (no ack, cursor not advanced) that hits step 0 → `already_processed`, acked silently, and the ratchet state is re-derived on the next genuine message via `MKSKIPPED` (the message key for `n` was never committed as consumed, so `SkipMessageKeys` stores it and it is evicted later); a crash between (b) and (c) also leads to redelivery and hits step 0; a crash after (c) leaves the ack in `pendingAcks` and it is sent on next launch. All three cases are in the `replay` scenario of `ratchet_conversation.json`.
4. On any other failure the envelope is classified **undecryptable** with a reason code (`bad_envelope`, `unknown_sid`, `identity_mismatch`, `lookup_failed`, `key_change_expired`, `parked_overflow`, `replay`, `opk_gone`, `spk_gone`, `too_many_skipped`, `decrypt_failed`); a local system row "Bu mesaj çözülemedi (<reason>)" is inserted, the reason is logged in Diagnostics, and the envelope is acked. Decryption of a persisted failure is never retried.
5. Every fetched envelope is acked exactly once, after it is stored, parked-then-resolved, or classified. **`pendingAcks[]` is not specific to step 3(c): every ack, whatever its reason — `stored` (3(c)), `already_processed` (step 0), an undecryptable classification (step 4, including the expiry of a parked envelope), `parked_overflow` (step 2) or parked-then-resolved — is appended to `index.json`'s `pendingAcks[]` in the same atomic write that records that outcome and advances `cursor`** (for a parked envelope the cursor advanced at parking time and the ack is appended by the write that records the resolution); **acks are only ever sent from that list, and an id is removed only on server confirmation** (3(d)). A `msg_id` that is still parked is not in `pendingAcks[]` (never ack while parked). **Cursor** = max server `id` of envelopes that were stored, parked or classified (persisted in `index.json`, §5.3); parked envelopes advance the cursor because they are already on the device.

### 3.3 Double Ratchet (Signal §3, ChaChaPoly instantiation)

| Function | Definition |
|---|---|
| `KDF_RK(rk, dh)` | `HKDF(ikm = dh, salt = rk, info = "Kilit_DR_v1_RootChain", L = 64)` → `rk' = out[0:32]`, `ck = out[32:64]` |
| `KDF_CK(ck)` | `mk = HMAC-SHA256(ck, 0x01)`, `ck' = HMAC-SHA256(ck, 0x02)` |
| message keys | `HKDF(ikm = mk, salt = zeros, info = "Kilit_DR_v1_MessageKeys", L = 44)` → `key = out[0:32]`, `nonce = out[32:44]` |
| `ENCRYPT` | `ChaChaPoly.seal(pt, key, nonce, aad = CONCAT(AD, sid, header))`; wire = `ciphertext || tag(16)`; nonce implicit |
| `HEADER` | 41 bytes: `0x01 || dh_pub(32) || u32BE(pn) || u32BE(n)`; `hdr[0] != 0x01` → `badHeader` before any KDF |
| `CONCAT(ad, sid, h)` | `u16BE(len(ad)) || ad || sid(8) || h` — the session's 8-byte `sid` (§3.2; for `prekey` envelopes the receiver uses the `sid` it recomputed from `ek`, never the carried field) is inside the AAD, so a server that rewrites the plaintext `sid` can only make the envelope fail to decrypt. The `aad` field of every ratchet vector step is this full byte string |
| limits | `MAX_SKIP = 1000` per chain (`Nr + MAX_SKIP < until` → `tooManySkipped`); `MKSKIPPED` eviction below |

State `{DHs (own private key copy), DHr, RK, CKs, CKr, Ns, Nr, PN, MKSKIPPED}`; `RatchetEncrypt / RatchetDecrypt / TrySkippedMessageKeys / SkipMessageKeys / DHRatchet` follow the spec pseudocode verbatim (`Nr += 1` after deriving `mk`; `DHRatchet` resets `Ns/Nr`, two `KDF_RK` steps). `GENERATE_DH` goes through a `KeySource` protocol (`RandomKeySource` in the app, `ScriptedKeySource` in tests). **Private-key hygiene:** `DHs` is held in a `ZeroizingKey` wrapper (Swift: `SymmetricKey`-backed storage that is zeroised on deallocation, never plain `Data`; Go: `defer clear(buf)`); `DHRatchet` overwrites and zeroises the previous `DHs`. `RatchetInitBob` seeds `DHs` with the SPK keypair; the `DHRatchet` performed while *decrypting the initiator's first message* (A1: `header.dh != DHr` with `DHr = None`, inside the very decrypt that creates the session, before any send) replaces and zeroises it, so a committed responder state never contains an SPK private key — there is no send-time special case and no extra SPK copy in the state (the `basic` vector asserts `expect_state_excludes` after the first decrypt, i.e. after A1 and therefore after B1). The 28-day SPK retention of §3.1 lives only in `PrekeyStore`. **Invariants (ChaCha20-Poly1305 has no nonce-misuse resistance):** decrypt works on a copy of the state and commits only after `open` succeeds (§3.2b order); encrypt persists the advanced state atomically *before* ciphertext is handed to the network; a retry resends the cached envelope, never re-encrypts with the same `mk` (a `ctrl.resync` resend is a *new* encryption of old plaintext, not a reuse).

**MKSKIPPED eviction (exact, so both sides agree on the remaining key set):** an entry `(dh_pub, n) → (mk, insertedAt)` belongs to the chain identified by its `dh_pub`. **Total order (so ties never diverge):** entries are ordered by `(insertedAt, dh_pub bytes lexicographically, n)` ascending; all entries inserted by one `SkipMessageKeys` call share one `insertedAt` taken once at call start (so a single call inserting up to 1000 entries creates ties by design). On every insert: (1) drop entries older than 7 days; (2) while more than 5 distinct chains remain, drop every entry of the chain whose *newest* entry is smallest in that order; (3) while more than 2000 entries remain, drop the first entry in that order. An entry is deleted the moment it is used. `ratchet_eviction.json` (§3.8) contains the eviction scenario asserting the exact remaining key set; it deliberately contains `insertedAt` ties across chains and within one call.

Padding: inner JSON (UTF-8) → `json || 0x80 || 0x00*k` to the next multiple of 256 bytes (ISO/IEC 7816-4; at least one byte is always added); unpadding rejects a block whose length is zero or not a multiple of 256 (a padded block is at least 256 bytes; `len == 0` → `badPadding`), whose last non-zero byte is not `0x80`, or that is all zero (`KilitCryptoError.badPadding`); at the envelope level a `ct` shorter than 256 + 16 bytes is rejected as `bad_envelope` before any KDF (§3.2b step 1). `envelope.json` `padding_negative` includes the empty block. **Size budget:** inner JSON ≤ 40 960 bytes before padding (rejected before encryption), so padded ≤ 41 216 B, ciphertext ≤ 41 232 B, base64url ≤ 54 976 chars; with `hdr`, `sid`, the `x3dh` block (~300 chars) and framing the decoded envelope stays below the 65 536-byte envelope cap. The server decodes `envelope` and rejects `len(decoded) > maxEnvelope` with `413 envelope_too_large`; the HTTP body cap (128 KiB for `POST /v1/messages`) is enforced separately. `envelope.json` contains the maximum-size case and a `len % 256 != 0` negative.

### 3.4 Envelope formats

Server-visible `POST /v1/messages` body (the server treats `envelope` as opaque bytes and supplies `from` itself on delivery; there is no client-supplied `from`):
```json
{"to":"bob","msg_id":"9f1c…32hex","envelope":"<base64url(E2E bytes)>"}
```
E2E envelope bytes = UTF-8 JSON (`b64` = base64url, no padding; `sid` on every envelope; the server can read every field below except `ct`):
```json
{"v":1,"kind":"prekey","sid":"b64(8)",
 "x3dh":{"ik_sig":"b64","ik_dh":"b64","ik_bind_sig":"b64","ek":"b64","spk_id":7,"opk_id":1203},
 "hdr":"b64(41 bytes)","ct":"b64(ciphertext||tag)"}
```
`kind` is `"prekey"` (with `x3dh`; `opk_id` may be `null` or absent) or `"msg"` (no `x3dh`). Inner plaintext JSON (after unpadding); `ttl` is the conversation timer at send time in seconds (0 = off) and is present on every message so disappearing deadlines are reorder-safe:
```json
{"t":"text","id":"uuid","ts":1760000000,"ttl":86400,"text":"merhaba"}
{"t":"image","id":"uuid","ts":1760000000,"ttl":86400,
 "att":{"blob":"32hex","key":"b64(fileKey 32)","sha256":"b64(32)","mime":"image/jpeg","len":123456,"w":1200,"h":900}}
{"t":"ctrl.timer","id":"uuid","ts":…,"ttl":0,"timer":3600}
{"t":"ctrl.capture","id":"uuid","ts":…,"ttl":0,"seq_from":42,"seq_to":44,"count":3,
 "kind":"screenshot","ref":["uuid","uuid"]}        // kind: screenshot|recording_started|recording_stopped|blanking_unavailable
{"t":"ctrl.read","id":"uuid","ts":…,"ttl":0,"ref":["uuid"]}
{"t":"ctrl.delete","id":"uuid","ts":…,"ttl":0,"ref":["uuid"]}
{"t":"ctrl.resync","id":"uuid","ts":…,"ttl":0,"lost_sid":"b64(8)"}
```
Control-message semantics and budget (also in `docs/PROTOCOL.md`):
- **Dedupe and `ref` resolution.** The dedupe key is `(author, id)`. `ref` lists are resolved as `(expected author, id)`: for `ctrl.delete` the expected author is the control's sender; for `ctrl.read` the expected author is the receipt's recipient (i.e. the local user); for `ctrl.capture` the expected author is either party (`ref` is informational). An `id` that collides with a message of the other author is simply a different message. `ratchet_conversation.json` scenario `id_collision` covers it.
- `ctrl.capture`: events are **coalesced** client-side into one message per 2 s window per `kind`, carrying the **per-conversation** monotonic counter (owned by `CaptureGlue`, persisted in `index.json` as `captureSeq[peer]`) as the range `seq_from…seq_to` (gaps remain visible to the receiver), `count`, and the merged `ref` list of message ids visible during the window. **Field presence by `kind`:** `seq_from`, `seq_to`, `count` and `ref` are present for `screenshot`, `recording_started` and `recording_stopped`, and each such event consumes one `seq`; for `blanking_unavailable` the three counters are omitted (or `null` — absent and `null` are equivalent, §3), `ref` is `[]`, and it does **not** advance `seq` (so it never creates a "Bildirilmeyen olay" gap on a receiver). **Swift types (three packages that must stay independent):** the inner-message `Codable` in `Crypto/Envelope.swift` (WP5) declares the inner `kind` field as a plain `String` carrying exactly the wire spellings `screenshot|recording_started|recording_stopped|blanking_unavailable`, and the three counters as optionals; `Security/CaptureEventKind.swift` (WP6) is `enum CaptureEventKind: String, Codable { case screenshot, recordingStarted = "recording_started", recordingStopped = "recording_stopped", blankingUnavailable = "blanking_unavailable" }` with the **same raw values**; `App/CaptureGlue.swift` (WP8) converts between the two with `rawValue` / `init(rawValue:)` (an unknown spelling from the wire maps to `nil`, is logged in Diagnostics and renders no row). `Crypto/` therefore never imports `Security/` and vice versa. The receiver persists the highest `seq_to` seen per peer as `lastPeerCaptureSeq[peer]` in `conv/<peer>.json` (§5.3) to render gaps. Control messages are queued at lower priority than text/image messages and sent after them.
- `ctrl.read`: optional (Settings "Okundu bilgisi", default **off**). Sent at most once per 5 s per conversation, batching the ids first revealed in that window; only ids authored by the *recipient of the receipt* are honoured; renders a small "Görüldü" state on the sender.
- `ctrl.delete`: sent **only for an explicit user action** ("Benim için ve karşı taraf için sil", §10.3) on a message the local user authored — never by a timer (sender-side expiry is local and silent, §5.8). `ref` entries must identify messages **authored by the sender of the control message**; the receiver deletes those messages + blobs + keys locally; any other id is ignored and logged.
- `ctrl.resync`: sent as the first inner message of a fresh session created under S3, on a bounded `unknown_sid ∈ known_sids` trigger, or by the rejected-initiation path of §3.2 step 3. The receiver marks the session `lost_sid` superseded **even if it has no `lastActivity`** (this is the case for an own-initiated session whose first message was rejected by the peer; S1 covers the sessions that do have one), re-evaluates its sending session per the rule in §3.2, and **re-sends on the new session** its own messages that were sent on `lost_sid` within the last 7 days and are still stored locally (oldest first, at most 50), with their original `id`/`ts`/`ttl` so the peer's `(author, id)` dedupe drops any that had arrived. Each resend is a fresh ratchet encryption.
- `blanking_unavailable`: sent once per app launch per peer, the first time a conversation with that peer is opened while `SecureCanvasView.isProtected == false`.
- Every control message is a full ratchet message (advances the sending chain, 256-byte-padded envelope); the budget above keeps a screenshot burst, a 20 s reveal cycle or a 50-message resync inside the server's `Every(100ms)`/burst-50 send limit (the resync is paced at 10/s).

### 3.5 Attachments (single-shot, phase 1)

`blob_id` = 16 random bytes generated **by the client** (hex32 on the wire; the server rejects a duplicate id with `409 blob_exists`, on which the client generates a **new `blob_id` and a new `fileKey`**, re-derives `key || nonce`, re-seals the plaintext and re-uploads, at most 3 attempts). **A `fileKey` is never used for more than one `seal` call:** the AEAD key and nonce are derived solely from `fileKey` and the AAD contains `blob_id`, so re-sealing the same plaintext under the same `fileKey` with a different `blob_id` would produce two Poly1305 tags under one one-time key — a nonce reuse that lets the server (which sees both bodies) forge tags for bit-flipped ciphertext. `AttachmentCrypto.seal` / `kilitcrypto.SealAttachment` therefore draw `fileKey` from an injected byte source (`KeySource`-style, `RandomKeySource` in the app) on every call and never accept a caller-supplied key; a `pkg/kilitcrypto` test asserts that two seals of the same plaintext yield distinct `(key, nonce)`, and WP7's `AttachmentUploadTests` (stubbed `APIClient` returning `409` once) asserts the retry path uploads a different `blob_id`, a different `att.key` and a different `att.sha256`. `fileKey` = 32 random bytes = the `att.key` field. `HKDF(ikm = fileKey, salt = zeros, info = "Kilit_Attach_v1", L = 44)` → `key || nonce`. `AAD = "Kilit_Attach_v1" || version(1 byte, 0x01) || blob_id(16) || u16BE(len(mime)) || mime || u64BE(plaintext_len)`, where `plaintext_len` = `att.len` (the receiver must find exactly that many bytes after `open`). **The AAD deliberately omits sender and recipient:** the `fileKey` travels only inside the ratchet message, so binding to parties adds nothing and would complicate the vectors. Blob file = `"KATT" || 0x01 || ciphertext || tag(16)` (plaintext + 21 bytes); `att.sha256` = SHA-256 over the **entire blob file including the 5-byte header**. Version `0x02` is reserved for the chunked format (phase 1.5): `HKDF(ikm = fileKey, salt = zeros, info = "Kilit_Attach_v2", L = 36)` → `key(32) || prefix(4)`; 1 MiB chunks, nonce = `prefix(4) || u64BE(i)`, AAD = v1 AAD with `0x02` version byte `|| u32BE(i) || u8(is_last)`; v1 and v2 keys are therefore distinct.

**Send pipeline:** the picked `UIImage` is normalised by drawing it into `UIGraphicsImageRenderer(size: targetSize, format: {scale = 1, opaque = true})` with the long edge ≤ 2048 px and calling `jpegData(compressionQuality: 0.8)` on the result — this output is upright, carries no EXIF orientation tag and no GPS/maker metadata, and `att.w/h` equal the pixel dimensions. Plaintext cap 8 MiB. Upload precedes the message; the message carries `blob`, `key`, `sha256`, `len`, `mime`, `w`, `h`. **The sender never deletes the blob** (it cannot know whether delivery happened; its own local expiry may precede delivery); the server's blob TTL (§4.7) is the sender-side bound.

> **Reviewer note (sender-side blob deletion).** Having the sender call `DELETE /v1/attachments/{id}` when its own message expires is rejected: sender-side expiry is `sentAt + ttl` (e.g. 30 s) and happens before an offline recipient fetches the message, which would turn every short-timer attachment into "Ek süresi doldu". The recipient-side delete below plus the server TTL implements ack-based deletion.

**§3.5b Receive and display pipeline (feature 2):** `dataTask` on the ephemeral REST session (no `downloadTask`, so no temp file in `Caches/`) → verify `sha256` over the received bytes → write ciphertext with `[.atomic, .completeFileProtection]` to `blobs/<id>.bin` (a 404 shows "Ek süresi doldu" instead of an error) → **issue `DELETE /v1/attachments/{id}`** (fire-and-forget, retried from the outbox with the same 5 s–10 min backoff; a 404 counts as success). On open: read the file, allocate an owned `UnsafeMutableRawBufferPointer` of `att.len` bytes, `ChaChaPoly.open` with the AAD above and copy the result into that buffer (the transient `Data` returned by `open` is released immediately; it is the one copy that cannot be scrubbed), check `count == att.len`, wrap the buffer with `CFDataCreateWithBytesNoCopy(kCFAllocatorDefault, ptr, len, kCFAllocatorNull)`, `CGImageSourceCreateWithData(cfData, [kCGImageSourceShouldCache: false] as CFDictionary)`, then `CGImageSourceCreateThumbnailAtIndex(src, 0, [kCGImageSourceCreateThumbnailFromImageAlways: true, kCGImageSourceCreateThumbnailWithTransform: true, kCGImageSourceThumbnailMaxPixelSize: 2048, kCGImageSourceShouldCache: false] as CFDictionary)` (both functions take `CFDictionary?`, so the `as CFDictionary` bridge is required; ### 3.6 Safety number

Per party: `h = SHA256("Kilit_SafetyNumber_v1" || IK_sig || IK_dh || username)`, then 5199 times `h = SHA256(h || IK_sig || IK_dh)`. Take `h[0:30]` as six 5-byte big-endian integers, each `mod 100000`, zero-padded to 5 digits → 30 digits. Safety number = 60 digits, lexicographically smaller 30-digit string first; shown in 12 groups of 5 and as QR `kilit-sn:v1:<60 digits>`. **The keys used are the identity keys bound in the active session's AD**, which the code asserts equal the contact record (`identityMismatch` otherwise). Any change of a contact's `IK_sig`/`IK_dh` — detected on fetch *or* in a `prekey` envelope (§3.2 step 1) — shows a blocking "Güvenlik numarası değişti" banner and disables sending until the user taps "Yeni anahtarı kabul et".

### 3.7 Local store wrapping

`masterKey = SymmetricKey(size: .bits256)` in Keychain. `storeKey = HKDF(ikm = masterKey, salt = zeros, info = "Kilit_LocalStore_v1", L = 32)`; per document `docKey = HKDF(ikm = storeKey, salt = zeros, info = "Kilit_LocalStoreDoc_v1" || relativePath, L = 32)`. Each store document = `ChaChaPoly.seal(JSON, docKey, nonce: random(4) || u64BE(generation), authenticating: AAD).combined` with `AAD = "Kilit_LocalStore_v1" || relativePath (UTF-8) || u64BE(generation)`, written `[.atomic, .completeFileProtection]`. `(docKey, nonce)` uniqueness is structural: distinct paths have distinct keys, and `generation` is strictly increasing per path (the 4 random bytes are a bonus, not a requirement). `generation` is a per-document counter incremented on every write; the counters for all documents live in `index.json`, and the generation of `index.json` itself lives in the Keychain item `store.generation`.

**`blobs/<id>.bin` is NOT a store document:** it holds the attachment ciphertext exactly as downloaded (already AEAD-protected by `fileKey`, §3.5), has no generation counter and no `docKey`, is written `[.atomic, .completeFileProtection]`, and its only integrity check is `att.sha256` verified on download and again on open (a mismatch shows "Ek süresi doldu" and deletes the file); `LocalStoreError.rollback` never applies to it. **Normative write order and load rule (applies to `conv/*.json` and `sessions/*.json` only):** (1) child documents (`conv/*.json`, `sessions/*.json`) first, each `[.atomic]`; (2) then `index.json`; (3) then the Keychain `store.generation`, updated on a serial background queue, coalesced to at most once per 2 s and flushed on `sceneDidEnterBackground`. On load a document is accepted when its `generation ≥` the recorded value (**never** equality); a higher value is adopted as the new record (a crash after step 1 or 2 is therefore harmless). A document whose generation is *below* the recorded value, or whose AAD does not match its path, fails closed (`LocalStoreError.rollback`): for a session file this deletes the session and triggers S3 (§3.2), for `index.json` a wipe prompt. A Keychain write that fails after a successful `index.json` write is tolerated (next load sees `≥`). This catches file swaps, restored-from-backup copies and accidental rollback (which would otherwise lead to message-key/nonce reuse on the next send). `SessionStoreAtomicityTests` injects a crash after each of the three writes and asserts no wipe prompt and no forced X3DH.

`AUTOINCREMENT` on `users.id` and `messages.id` prevents rowid reuse after `DELETE /v1/users/me`; `sender_username` is denormalised at insert time so `from` on delivery is resolvable after the sender's account is gone (store test: delete user → re-register same name → old queued rows are delivered with the old `from` and are undecryptable by the new identity). Atomic OPK claim (inside the same immediate tx as the `consumed_opks` insert): `DELETE FROM one_time_prekeys WHERE id=(SELECT id FROM one_time_prekeys WHERE user_id=? ORDER BY id LIMIT 1) RETURNING key_id, pub`. Signed prekeys: bundle fetch returns `ORDER BY key_id DESC LIMIT 1` (incl. `created_at`); `PUT /v1/prekeys` with `spk` rejects `key_id <= current` (`400 spk_not_monotonic`), inserts, then deletes all but the two newest rows for that user. OPK upload is transactional: a `key_id` that is already stored **or present in `consumed_opks`** for the same user → `409 opk_exists` (whole request rejected — otherwise a re-uploaded id could be claimed twice within 30 days and the second claim would fail on the `consumed_opks` primary key inside the claim transaction, turning a bundle fetch into a 500; store test `TestReuploadConsumedOPKRejected`); more than 500 stored OPKs after the insert → `400 too_many_opks`. `consumed_opks` rows are swept after 30 days. `CreateSession` deletes older `sessions` rows of the same `(user_id, device_id)` and caps the table at 10 rows per user (oldest `created_at` evicted), so repeated logins cannot grow it without bound. **Idempotent message insert (one immediate tx):** `SELECT id, sender_id, recipient_id FROM messages WHERE msg_id=?` → row with the same sender and recipient → `202` with that `id`; row with a different sender or recipient → `409 msg_id_conflict`; no row → check queue caps (`SELECT count(*) … WHERE recipient_id=?` and `… AND sender_id=?`) → `INSERT … RETURNING id`.

### 4.3 REST endpoints

#### 4.3.1 Conventions and error catalogue
- All timestamps (`ts`, `expires_at`, `created_at`, `server_time`) are unix seconds as JSON integers.
- Key bytes, `nonce`, `token`, `sig`, `envelope` are base64url unpadded strings; `msg_id` and `blob_id` are 32 lowercase hex chars; `device_id` is a lowercase UUIDv4; `username` is canonical (§3). Optional fields: absent and `null` are equivalent (§3).
- Errors are `{"error":"<code>","message":"<human text>"}` with the HTTP status in the table. Unknown users on `challenge` are indistinguishable from known ones (a real nonce is always issued for every *syntactically valid* username, bound to the username string; a malformed username is `400 bad_username`). `404 unknown_user` is returned by `GET /v1/users/{u}`, `GET /v1/users/{u}/prekeys`, `POST /v1/messages` (unknown `to`) and `POST /v1/attachments/{id}` (unknown recipient); `404 not_found` by `GET`/`DELETE /v1/attachments/{id}`. The `ServeMux` automatic 405 (method mismatch) and the unknown-route 404 are plain text, not JSON; clients key on the status code only.
- Body caps are per route (middleware): JSON routes 64 KiB, `POST /v1/messages` 128 KiB, `PUT /v1/prekeys` 256 KiB, attachment upload `-max-upload` (8 MiB + 21). `http.MaxBytesHandler` is **not** applied to the whole mux. `POST /v1/messages/ack` and the WS `ack` frame accept at most 500 `msg_ids` (`400 bad_json` / `bad_frame` beyond that).
- Boolean config values given through `KILIT_*` env vars accept `1/true/yes` and `0/false/no` (`strconv.ParseBool`, §4.8).

### 4.4 WebSocket protocol (text frames, JSON, `SetReadLimit(128<<10)`, ≤ 3 connections/user)

Client → server: `{"t":"hello","after":4400}` (must be the first frame and arrive within 10 s of the upgrade, else close `1008` reason `no_hello`), `{"t":"ack","msg_ids":[…]}` (≤ 500 ids, else `bad_frame`; fire-and-forget, idempotent; REST ack also allowed), `{"t":"ping"}`.
Server → client: `{"t":"msg","id":4411,"msg_id":"…","from":"alice","envelope":"…","ts":1760000000}`, `{"t":"pong"}`, `{"t":"prekeys_low","opk_count":12,"consumed_ids":[…]}` (sent on connect when the count is below 20 — the hub calls `store.PrekeyCount` for the principal — and whenever `Notifier.PrekeysLow` is called by the API after a claim; non-blocking, same 256-slot queue, dropped if full), `{"t":"error","code":"bad_frame|rate_limited"}`.
**Drain/live sequencing (per connection):** after `hello` the connection is in `draining`; `Notify` deliveries arriving during the drain are buffered (same 256-slot queue); the drain pages `id > after` in pages of 100 (`store.ListMessages(ctx, uid, after, 100)` → `ws.Delivery`, `MsgID` hex-encoded) in order; when the last page is sent, buffered deliveries with `id ≤ lastDrained` are dropped and the rest are flushed in `id` order; then the connection is `live`. A `ws` test inserts a row concurrently with the drain and asserts strictly increasing `id`s with no gap and no duplicate; a second test asserts that **a row with `id ≤ after` is never re-sent**, acked or not (the client's persisted `pendingAcks` is the only recovery for a lost ack, §3.2b).
Limits: a 4th connection for a user closes the *oldest* with `1000` reason `superseded`; a frame over the read limit makes the library close the connection with `1009` automatically (the WS test asserts `CloseStatus == 1009`); an unparseable frame → error frame then close `1002`; a full per-connection queue → close `1008` reason `slow_consumer`; **frames are rate-limited per user, never per connection** (`ack`/`ping`: `Every(200ms)`, burst 30, from one `ratelimit.Keyed` keyed by user id that the hub constructs itself, so the bucket is shared across a user's ≤ 3 connections; over the limit → close `1008` reason `rate_limited` on the connection that sent the frame) and the upgrade itself is limited (§4.6), so one authenticated client cannot starve the `_txlock=immediate` writers with `ack` deletes. Sending is REST-only. Server pings every 30 s via `Conn.Ping(ctx, 10 s)`, closes after two failures. Handlers read with `context.Background()`; shutdown uses `RegisterOnShutdown(hub.closeAll)` → `Close(StatusGoingAway, "server shutting down")` → `WaitGroup`.

### 4.5 Auth flow

Challenge: 32 random bytes; store `SHA256(nonce)` bound to the canonical `username` string, `expires_at = now+60`; respond with `server_time` so the client computes `offset = server_time − localNow` and signs with `ts = localNow + offset`. Register/login/delete: `|now − ts| ≤ 300` (else `401 clock_skew` with `server_time`); consume with `DELETE FROM auth_nonces WHERE nonce_hash=? AND username=? AND expires_at>? RETURNING 1` (0 rows → `401 nonce_invalid`) *before* verifying; rebuild the signed message server-side from the fields plus `-public-origin`; `ed25519.Verify` after the strict pre-checks of §3. Register verifies `register_proof` with the submitted `ik_sig` and issues a token directly. Token: 32 random bytes, stored as SHA-256, 30-day expiry; the bearer middleware calls `store.LookupSession` on every request and `store.TouchSession` when `now − LastSeen ≥ 60` (so `last_seen` is updated at most once per minute per token), then stores `authctx.Principal{UserID, Username}` in the context. The client maps `401 invalid_token` → re-challenge/login once; `401 clock_skew` → explanation "Cihaz saati yanlış" (never re-onboarding); persistent `401 bad_signature`/`unknown_user` on login → `.onboarding` with explanation.

### 4.6 Rate limits (`internal/ratelimit.Keyed`; eviction is per limiter, see below)

`ratelimit.NewKeyed(r rate.Limit, burst int)` computes its own idle threshold `idle = burst × interval` (register: 3 × 20 min = 60 min; delete: 3 h; every other row ≤ 10 min, floored at 10 min) and its janitor evicts an entry only after it has been idle at least that long — i.e. only when its bucket would be full again anyway. (A fixed 10-minute eviction would recreate the register bucket full, turning "3 per hour" into "3 per 10 minutes".) API test: 3 registers from one IP, advance the injected clock by 11 min, the 4th register is still `429`.

> **Reviewer note (r3, register `Reserve()`/`Cancel()` removed).** Revision 2 refunded the register token on `409 username_taken` with `Reserve()` + `Cancel()`. In `x/time/rate` v0.14.0 a zero-delay reservation has `timeToAct == now`, and `CancelAt` returns early when `timeToAct.Before(t)`, so a `Cancel()` issued after the handler ran restores nothing; three taken names would lock an IP out for 20 minutes. The availability check now precedes the limiter instead; the test name `register 409 does not consume the limiter` is kept. **Client IP behind the proxy:** `X-Forwarded-For` (first untrusted hop) is honored only when `RemoteAddr` matches a `-trusted-proxy` CIDR; otherwise `RemoteAddr` is used. If a request carries `X-Forwarded-For` while no trusted proxy is configured, the middleware logs a warning (once per minute) "requests arrive via a proxy but -trusted-proxy is unset; per-IP limits are shared". `docker-compose.yml` configures the proxy CIDR (§4.9). API tests: with a trusted proxy configured, two `X-Forwarded-For` values get separate buckets; without, the header is ignored. API test (the quota is enforced by `internal/ratelimit` + the handler through the `claimOPK` argument, so the store cannot observe it): 50 bundle fetches of one target by one account leave ≥ 90 of its 100 OPKs. Store test: `FetchBundle(claimOPK=false)` never deletes an OPK row.

### 4.7 Attachment storage and sweepers

`internal/blob.Store` (WP2, contract in §4.1) wraps `os.OpenRoot(blobDir)` (Go 1.24 method set only: `OpenFile, Open, Mkdir, Remove, Stat`). Path `id[:2]/id`; `Create` = `OpenFile(O_CREATE|O_EXCL|O_WRONLY, 0o600)` (`fs.ErrExist` → `blob.ErrExists` → 409); the handler `io.Copy`s from `http.MaxBytesReader(w, r.Body, maxUpload)` applied only on this route (`*http.MaxBytesError` → 413), `Sync`s, then inserts the row; a failed insert removes the file. Serve with `http.ServeContent`, `application/octet-stream`, `Cache-Control: no-store`. **Division of labour (one sweeper, file before row):** `cmd/kilitd` runs a single 60 s ticker that calls `store.Sweep` (expired messages, nonces, sessions, `consumed_opks` — rows only) and then `store.ExpiredAttachments(now, 500)` → for each id `blobs.Remove(id)` → `store.DeleteAttachment(id)` (file first, so a crash leaves at most an orphan *row*, which is retried next tick, never an orphan file). There is no `internal/blob/sweeper.go`. `DELETE /v1/users/me` likewise removes the owner's files (`ListAttachmentsByOwner` → `blobs.Remove`) before `DeleteUser` cascades the rows. **Blob TTL = message TTL + 24 h** (`-blob-ttl` defaults to `-msg-ttl + 24h`; startup refuses `blob-ttl < msg-ttl + 24h`), because a blob is always uploaded *before* the message that references it and the gap between upload and send is unbounded; a recipient that still hits a 404 shows "Ek süresi doldu". Startup `blobs.Reconcile(keep)` (`filepath.WalkDir` under the root) removes orphan files whose id has no `attachments` row. API tests: an 8 MiB upload succeeds and a 8 MiB + 22 byte upload is 413; upload → download (recipient) → `DELETE` (recipient) → `GET` returns 404 and the file is gone.

### 5.4 Networking

Two `URLSessionConfiguration.ephemeral` sessions, both with `urlCache = nil`, `requestCachePolicy = .reloadIgnoringLocalCacheData`: the **REST/upload session** with `waitsForConnectivity = true`, `timeoutIntervalForRequest = 60` (default; idle gap between bytes) and `timeoutIntervalForResource = 600` (the whole transfer, which must accommodate an 8 MiB upload on a ~1 Mbit/s uplink plus time spent waiting for connectivity); the small JSON calls additionally set `URLRequest.timeoutInterval = 60` so a hung control request still fails within a minute, while `upload(blobID:recipient:data:)` and `download` leave the request timeout at the session default. The **WebSocket session** has `waitsForConnectivity = false`, `timeoutIntervalForRequest` left at its 60 s default (it also governs idle gaps between received frames, and the server pings only every 30 s) and **`timeoutIntervalForResource` left at its default** — a `URLSessionWebSocketTask` is a task that never completes while the socket is open, so a short resource timeout would tear down every healthy socket (error −1001) and the live path would never exist. The connect/handshake phase is bounded client-side instead: when `resume()` is called, `WebSocketClient` starts `let watchdog = Task { try await Task.sleep(for: .seconds(15)); task.cancel(with: .abnormalClosure, reason: nil) }` and cancels the watchdog when the first frame after `hello` (any server frame) arrives; a watchdog firing counts as a failed attempt for the backoff/poll state machine, so a dead LAN/VPN still fails fast. `WebSocketClientTests` stubs the watchdog (injected clock/duration) instead of a resource timeout.

> **Reviewer note (r3).** Revision 2 set `timeoutIntervalForResource = 15` on the WebSocket session as a connect timeout. Apple's documentation defines the resource timer as running "until either the request completes or this timeout interval is reached", so it is a lifetime cap, not a connect timeout; replaced by the watchdog above. `APIClient` methods mirror §4.3: `challenge`, `register`, `login`, `logout`, `deleteAccount`, `lookup(username:)`, `fetchBundle(username:)`, `uploadPrekeys`, `prekeyCount`, `send`, `poll(after:)`, `ack`, `upload(blobID:recipient:data:)`, `download`, `deleteBlob`. Server URL is user-entered in onboarding, canonicalised to `server_origin` (§3), stored in `index.json`, and **read-only after registration** (changeable only through "Hesabı sil ve sıfırla", because `register_proof` is origin-bound). **ATS fact:** Apple documents that from iOS 17 ATS no longer allows connections to IP-address hosts by default and that `NSAllowsLocalNetworking = YES` re-enables them; the **Debug** build's `Info-Debug.plist` therefore carries `NSAppTransportSecurity → NSAllowsLocalNetworking = true` (no `NSAllowsArbitraryLoads`) so `http://<lan-ip>:8080` / `ws://` work for the two-phone LAN test. The Release build has no ATS exceptions and no pin; §6 documents how the owner adds `NSPinnedDomains` once the production domain's CA SPKI hash is known. `WebSocketClient` creates `URLSessionWebSocketTask` from a `URLRequest` with the bearer header, sends `hello` with `index.json`'s `cursor`, exponential backoff 1–30 s, polls every 20 s while down (`WebSocketClientTests` uses a stubbed failing task and asserts the backoff/poll fallback starts); inbound rows are deduplicated by `msg_id` across the WS and poll paths before entering the §3.2b pipeline. **Acks are durable:** every `msg_id` to be acked, whatever the reason (stored, `already_processed`, classified undecryptable, `parked_overflow`, parked-then-resolved), is appended to `index.json`'s `pendingAcks[]` in the same atomic write as the cursor (§3.2b step 5), and acks are sent only from that list and flushed through `POST /v1/messages/ack` (≤ 500 ids per call) on launch, on foreground and every 60 s; the WS `ack` frame is only an optimisation. Outbound: encrypt → persist state + cached envelope → `POST`; on `429 recipient_full` the message stays in the outbox with backoff; control messages are coalesced and queued behind content messages (§3.4); attachment `DELETE`s and pending acks share the outbox; on `409 blob_exists` the upload is re-sealed with a fresh `blob_id` **and** `fileKey` (§3.5, `AttachmentUploadTests`).

### 5.6 Capture detection flow

`CaptureMonitor` (`@MainActor @Observable`, in `Security/`, depends on nothing outside UIKit/SwiftUI): a `CaptureEnvironmentReader` view reads `@Environment(\.isSceneCaptured)` and calls `monitor.update(captured:)`; `SceneDelegate` additionally uses `registerForTraitChanges([UITraitSceneCaptureState.self])` on the window scene (`sceneCaptureState == .active`) for the cover. `UIApplication.userDidTakeScreenshotNotification` is observed with a 0.5 s debounce (UIKit can post twice). ### 5.7 Hold-to-reveal and partial reveal

`HoldToReveal` renders an opaque pre-rendered placeholder: **a constant 56 pt tall bubble** for text (never sized by content, which would leak length) and a constant **200×150 pt** panel for images, each with a "görmek için basılı tut" glyph; the real `Text`/image is not in the view tree, so nothing to deblur, nothing for VoiceOver or snapshots. On reveal the bubble expands to its real height with animations disabled (`withTransaction(Transaction(animation: nil))`), rows below shift and the revealed bubble keeps its top edge where it was; on release it collapses back to 56 pt. **Gesture = UIKit, not SwiftUI's `onLongPressGesture`** (SwiftUI's `LongPressGesture` *succeeds and ends* when `minimumDuration` elapses, so `onPressingChanged(false)` fires in the same frame as `perform`): `PressHoldView: UIViewRepresentable` installs

```swift
let lp = UILongPressGestureRecognizer(target: context.coordinator, action: #selector(Coordinator.handle(_:)))
lp.minimumPressDuration = 0.3
lp.allowableMovement = 8
lp.cancelsTouchesInView = false
lp.delaysTouchesBegan = false
view.addGestureRecognizer(lp)
```
`.began → onChange(true)` **unless the enclosing scroll view (walk `superview`s for the first `UIScrollView`) `isDragging || isDecelerating`**, in which case the recognizer is reset (`isEnabled = false; isEnabled = true`) and nothing is revealed; `.ended, .cancelled → onChange(false)`. (UIKit sends no action message for `.failed` — a press that fails before `minimumPressDuration` never produced `.began`, so no `onChange(true)` was issued and no release event is needed; the `switch` lists `.failed` only in its `default` arm.) `allowableMovement = 8` stays below `UIScrollView`'s pan-start distance so a drag that pauses never reveals. The content lives inside `SecureCanvasView`, whose `hitTest` forwarding delivers the touch. `RevealController` (`@Observable`, injected clock) allows one revealed bubble at a time (`revealedID`), auto-ends after 20 s, plays haptic `.soft`; `HoldToRevealTests` covers the single-reveal invariant, the 20 s auto-end, and the scroll guard with a stubbed `isDragging` provider. Images: in `ProtectedImageViewer` the display layer is never masked — an opaque mid-tone panel (`#3A3A3E`) is drawn *on top of* the viewer as a SwiftUI overlay with a rectangular cut-out of `max(120pt, height/4)` centred on the finger (`DragGesture(minimumDistance: 0)`), implemented as `ZStack { Rectangle().fill(panel); Rectangle().frame(band).blendMode(.destinationOut) }.compositingGroup()` so the capture-protected layer is untouched. First reveal starts the disappearing timer and (if enabled) queues `ctrl.read`. **Own sent messages use the same 56 pt / 200×150 pt placeholder and the same reveal gesture** (the sender's screen is equally capturable); a sender-side reveal does not queue `ctrl.read` and does not affect its `expiresAt` (`sentAt + ttl`, §5.8).

### 5.8 Disappearing messages

Per-conversation timer (off, 30 s, 5 m, 1 h, 24 h, 7 d; **default off**) set by either party via `ctrl.timer`, shown as a system row; every message carries its own `ttl`. **With `ttl = 0` a message never expires.** With `ttl > 0`: receiver `expiresAt = firstRevealedAt + ttl` once revealed, and unrevealed messages expire at `receivedAt + max(ttl, 24 h) + 7 d`; sender `expiresAt = sentAt + ttl` regardless of whether the receiver ever revealed it (sender-side expiry deletes locally only; the server row was acked at delivery). `DisappearingScheduler` (injected clock; `DisappearingSchedulerTests` cover all three deadline formulas, the `ttl = 0` never-expires case and purge ordering) keeps one `Timer` for the earliest deadline while foregrounded, purges on launch and `scenePhase == .active`, and deletes message + blob + key. **Sender-side expiry is local only and sends nothing.** `ctrl.delete` is sent only for an explicit user action ("Benim için ve karşı taraf için sil", §10.3) on a message the local user authored; timer-based expiry on each device uses its own deadline formula above, so a short timer never destroys a message on an offline recipient before it is read (the same reasoning as the §3.5 note on sender-side blob deletion).

### 5.9 Watermark overlay

Payload 16 bits = `tag(8) || day(4) || crc4(4)`: `tag = HKDF(ikm = WS, salt = zeros, info = "Kilit_WM_v1" || viewerUsername, L = 1)[0]`, `day = (unixSeconds / 86400) mod 16`. **CRC-4:** polynomial `x⁴ + x + 1` (`0x3`), non-reflected, MSB-first, init `0x0`, no final XOR, no augmentation, over the 12 bits `tag(8) || day(4)` fed MSB-first:
```
crc = 0
for bit in bits:                      # 12 bits, MSB first
    fb  = ((crc >> 3) & 1) ^ bit
    crc = ((crc << 1) & 0xF) ^ (0x3 if fb else 0)
```
A bit-by-bit worked example is in `watermark.json` (for `tag = 171`, `day = 7` the loop yields `crc = 4`, `payload16 = 43892`; the numbers in §3.9 are illustrative and the generated file is normative). **WS used for the mark:** the contact record stores `wm_secrets[] = [{sid, ws}]`, one entry for every session ever established with that peer (both sides append both sessions of a crossed initiation once each is committed, so the sets converge); the mark uses **`wm_secret` = the `ws` of the entry with the lexicographically smallest `sid`** (deterministic on both devices, the same tie-break as the sending rule; in a crossed initiation "first established" differs per device, so order of establishment is not usable). The list is never pruned and is reset only on an accepted identity-key change. Diagnostics shows all `wm_secrets` in hex (smallest `sid` first) so the owner can feed them to the decoder; the decoder accepts a list of candidate WS values and tries all, and `watermark.json` carries the single canonical `ws`.

Layout: tile 6×6 cells, cell 28 pt (168 pt tile). **Bit placement (normative for the Go generator, the Swift renderer and the decoder):** the 20 border cells carry the fixed sync sequence `0b10110100011100101001` placed in order on cells `(0,0)…(0,5)` (top row, left to right), then `(1,5)…(5,5)` (right column, downwards), then `(5,4)…(5,0)` (bottom row, right to left), then `(4,0)…(1,0)` (left column, upwards) — each corner used exactly once, 6 + 5 + 5 + 4 = 20 cells, indices `(row, col)`. The inner 4×4 carries the 16 payload bits row-major with **bit 15 (MSB of `tag`) in cell `(1,1)`, bit 14 in `(1,2)`, … bit 12 in `(1,4)`, bit 11 in `(2,1)`, … bit 0 (LSB of `crc`) in `(4,4)`**. Manchester-within-cell: bit 1 = left half brighter, right half darker; bit 0 reversed. The `cells` matrix in `watermark.json` is the result of this placement.

**Integer delta map (identical code in Go `watermark.go` and Swift `WatermarkPayload.swift`, no Core Graphics drawing, no filter API):** for scale `s ∈ {1,2,3}`, `W = H = 168·s`, `cell = 28·s`, `half = 14·s`, `k = 4·s` (box width, even). Base pattern `P[y][x] ∈ {−5, +5}`: cell `(r, c) = (y / cell, x / cell)`, bit `b` from the matrix, `left = (x mod cell) < half`, `P = +5` if `b == left` else `−5`. Softening = separable box filter with periodic wrap (so tiles are seamless): `Hs[y][x] = Σ_{i=−k/2}^{k/2−1} P[y][xi]` with **`xi = ((x + i) % W + W) % W`**, and `V[y][x] = Σ_{j=−k/2}^{k/2−1} Hs[yj][x]` with **`yj = ((y + j) % H + H) % H`** — `%` is truncating in both Swift and Go (`-2 % 168 == -2`), so the double-mod form is required and a plain `(x+i) % W` would index −2 and crash (or, with `abs`, break the seamless wrap); `Δ[y][x] = V / (k·k)` using **integer division truncating toward zero** (Swift `Int./` and Go `int /` agree). The map is `int8` with values in `[−5, 5]`, produced by `WatermarkPayload.deltaMap(scale:) -> [Int8]` (W·H row-major) in Swift and `watermark.DeltaMap(scale int) []int8` in Go. Two **opaque** BGRA images are derived per pixel: lighter `L = max(0, Δ)` in all three colour channels, alpha 255; darker `D = 255 − max(0, −Δ)`, alpha 255. With opaque sources `plusLighter` gives `R = min(1, dst + L/255)` and `plusDarker` gives `R = max(0, dst + D/255 − 1) = dst − max(0,−Δ)/255` exactly, with no premultiplication or alpha ramp involved. `WatermarkTile.make(delta: [Int8], width: Int, height: Int, scale: CGFloat) -> (lighter: CGImage, darker: CGImage)` (WP6, takes the raw map, no `Crypto/` import) builds each `CGImage` from a `Data` of `4·W·H` bytes (B, G, R, 255 per pixel) with `CGDataProvider(data: cfData)!` and

```swift
CGImage(width: W, height: H, bitsPerComponent: 8, bitsPerPixel: 32, bytesPerRow: 4 * W,
        space: CGColorSpaceCreateDeviceRGB(),
        bitmapInfo: CGBitmapInfo(rawValue: CGBitmapInfo.byteOrder32Little.rawValue | CGImageAlphaInfo.noneSkipFirst.rawValue),
        provider: provider, decode: nil, shouldInterpolate: false, intent: .defaultIntent)
```
— the parameter is declared `bitmapInfo: CGBitmapInfo`, and `CGImageAlphaInfo` is a separate enum, so the OptionSet literal `[.byteOrder32Little, CGImageAlphaInfo.premultipliedFirst]` does not compile; the raw-value OR is the form the research digest verified. `.noneSkipFirst` is correct because both tiles are opaque (the fourth byte is ignored). WP8 caches the pair per `(payload, displayScale)` and wires payload → tile → overlay/layers. `watermark.json` carries the full scale-1 map, SHA-256 of scales 2 and 3, and sampled pixels; `WatermarkTests` and the Go golden test compare **exactly**.

**Placement:** (1) message list — `WatermarkOverlay(lighter:darker:phase:visibleString:)` is the **last child of the `ZStack` inside the hosted content** (not an `.overlay` on the representable), so it composites against ordinary SwiftUI layers inside the same `UIHostingController`. Because `View.offset` translates an already-laid-out view without resizing it, a tiled image sized to the ZStack and then offset by `(dx, dy)` would leave an unmarked strip `dx` wide and `dy` tall along two edges; the tiles are therefore **over-sized by one tile before the offset**: `Image(decorative: lighter, scale: displayScale).resizable(resizingMode: .tile).blendMode(.plusLighter).padding(-168).offset(x: -phase.x, y: -phase.y)` and the same for `darker` with `.plusDarker`, where `phase` is a per-viewing-session random point in `[0,168)²`, both `.allowsHitTesting(false)`, `.accessibilityHidden(true)`, and the enclosing `ZStack` is `.clipped()`. (2) image viewer — `CaptureProtectedImageView` adds two `CALayer`s above its display layer (`WatermarkLayers(lighter:darker:phase:)`): `backgroundColor = UIColor(patternImage: UIImage(cgImage: tile, scale: displayScale, orientation: .up)).cgColor`, `compositingFilter = "plusL"` / `"plusD"` (the Core Animation filter names SwiftUI's `.plusLighter`/`.plusDarker` map to); each layer's `frame = view.bounds.insetBy(dx: -168, dy: -168)` (one tile larger on every side, in points) and the phase is applied by setting `layer.bounds.origin = phase` — **never by moving `frame.origin`** — with `masksToBounds = true` on the parent; SwiftUI blend modes are not used over the AV layer. `SecureCanvasTests`/`WatermarkTests` assert, for several random phases, that each overlay's frame fully contains the content bounds after the phase is applied (`overlay.frame.contains(content.bounds)`). **Whether the render server honours these composites over the secure canvas and over a `preventsCapture` layer cannot be sampled in-app** (snapshot APIs do not reflect render-server compositing), so it is a device-checklist item (§7: "watermark visible at ~5/255 over bubbles in a photo; no flat haze over the protected image") and a §9 risk; the fallback if either fails is the other variant (SwiftUI blend ↔ CALayer compositingFilter), both of which exist. Surfaces are mid-tone by design (bubbles `#4A4A4F` on `#2C2C30`, viewer letterbox `#3A3A3E`, never pure black). A visible diagonal "@ayse · 21 Eki" at 9 % alpha complements it. **Types crossing the WP5/WP6 boundary:** WP5 exposes `struct WatermarkPayload { let cells: [[Bool]]; func deltaMap(scale: Int) -> [Int8] }` (`Crypto/`); WP6's `WatermarkTile.make(delta:width:height:scale:)` takes the raw `[Int8]` map and returns the two `CGImage`s; `WatermarkOverlay(lighter:darker:phase:visibleString:)` and `WatermarkLayers(lighter:darker:phase:)` take the images; WP8 (`App/`) wires payload → tile → overlay/layers and owns the cache. Nothing in `Security/` sees the `Bool` matrix or imports `Crypto/` or `Store/`.

> **Reviewer note (pixel-sampling self-check).** A Diagnostics self-check that samples a live pixel is rejected: nothing an app can call reads the render server's composite of a secure canvas or a `preventsCapture` layer. Diagnostics instead verifies the delta-map → `CGImage` path offscreen (renders the two tiles over a `#4A4A4F` field with `UIGraphicsImageRenderer` and asserts the expected ±5 at sampled pixels), and the live composite is verified by the device photo checklist.

**Decoder (`server/cmd/wmdecode`, phase 1, core in `pkg/kilitcrypto/wmdecode.go`, pure Go):** inputs a JPEG/PNG and four screen-corner coordinates, perspective-warps (homography, bilinear sampling) to a canonical 1179×2556 grid, converts to luma, high-pass filters (subtract a separable box blur of radius one cell), integrates left/right half-cell means, searches tile phase (and ±5 % scale, ±3° rotation) by correlating the 20-cell sync sequence, rejects cells whose variance exceeds a threshold (text ink), majority-votes each data bit across tiles, checks CRC-4, and prints `{tag, day, confidence, matches:[username…]}` given `-ws` candidates and `-usernames`. Usage: `wmdecode -in photo.jpg -corners x1,y1,x2,y2,x3,y3,x4,y4 -ws <hex from Diagnostics>[,<hex>…] -usernames alice,bob` (corners clockwise from the screen's top-left as read from the cursor position in Preview.app). Go unit test: render the tile over a `#4A4A4F` field with sample text, box blur radius cell/3, JPEG Q75, resize 0.4× bilinear, decode → correct payload. Calibration knobs (only two): amplitude (3–6) and cell size (24–32 pt); device acceptance criteria are in §7.

### 5.11 Settings, app lock and onboarding

**App lock state machine** (`App/AppLock.swift`, `AppLockTests` with an injected authenticator): states `locked → authenticating → unlocked`. ## 9. Open risks and Mac-only verification

- **No Swift compiler on Linux**: every Swift file compiles for the first time on the Mac. Mitigations: only APIs checked against developer.apple.com, Swift 5 mode, no iOS 26-era overloads, smallest possible surface. Most likely first-build/first-run issues: `sizeThatFits` returning `nil`, `.environment(\.self, context.environment)` with `AnyView`, `registerForTraitChanges` closure typing, the `MediaAccessibility` import and `Notification.Name(rawValue: kMADimFlashingLightsChangedNotification as String)` bridge, `CGBitmapInfo(rawValue:)` for the tile images, the `CMSampleBuffer` creation sequence, `@Observable` + actor isolation warnings, `UIHostingController.safeAreaRegions` availability annotation, `UILongPressGestureRecognizer` target/selector plumbing, `CGImage(width:…provider:…)` argument labels, `UIColor(patternImage:)` scale handling, raw string literal size in the test target (split per set keeps each under 200 KB).
- **Secure canvas** (`_UITextLayoutCanvasView`): may be absent, renamed, recreated at runtime, or not blank on the owner's iOS; the continuously re-evaluated canary and amber badge make it visible; the `CaptureShield` layer-swap is the flag-selectable alternative; Apple DTS calls it "not its intended purpose" — App Review risk accepted and disclosed in `docs/APP_REVIEW_NOTES.md`.
- **`preventsCapture`** may make system recording fail to start instead of showing black; test and word the UI.
- **Watermark compositing**: whether `plusLighter`/`plusDarker` (SwiftUI, inside the hosted content) and `compositingFilter` `"plusL"`/`"plusD"` (CALayer, above the AV layer) survive render-server compositing over the secure canvas and a `preventsCapture` layer is unverifiable on Linux and unsampleable in-app; both variants exist and the device checklist decides. Display colour management on P3 panels may alter the ±5 code values by ±1 — irrelevant to the decoder, excluded from the exact test (which checks the delta map, not the panel).
- **CryptoKit/Go parity** is proven only when XCTest runs sets A and B; the likeliest divergences are CONCAT/header ordering, padding, AAD framing for attachments, the signed-message separators, `created_at` in the SPK bytes, the `opks_sig` batch layout, and base64url — each has a dedicated raw-byte vector.
- **120 Hz delivery** depends on the plist key being merged (both plists) and on the device; verify measured Hz in Diagnostics and every self-stop path.
- **Keychain `WhenPasscodeSetThisDeviceOnly`**: passcode removal deletes keys (forced re-registration); reinstall persistence; both device-only.
- **Watermark calibration** (amplitude 5, cell 28 pt) is a guess until photographed; the decoder exists in phase 1 so the owner can measure and tune.
- **Spec text** for X3DH/Double Ratchet was reconstructed from libsignal source and knowledge (signal.org unreachable from the build network); the owner should skim X3DH §2–4 and Double Ratchet §3/§5.2/§6 once before freezing the wire format.
- **Dependency pinning**: `x/crypto v0.48.0` is the last Go-1.24-compatible release; later fixes require raising the toolchain or switching the AEAD to stdlib AES-256-GCM (same `nonce||ct||tag` layout, version bump).
- **`URLSessionWebSocketTask` without an Origin header** against coder/websocket: expected fine; confirm on first device test.
- **iOS 26 full-screen screenshot preview** and **iPhone Mirroring**: document observed behaviour; no mitigation planned.
- **Multi-session convergence, supersession and `ctrl.resync`** are exercised by scripted vectors; the real crossed initiation and the reinstall/key-change flow on two phones are on the device checklist (§7 step 5).
