# Kilit wire protocol (normative summary of DESIGN.md §3)

This is the implementer's summary of the end-to-end protocol: byte layouts,
info strings, control-message semantics, the Ed25519 strictness rule and the
inbound policy. DESIGN.md §3 is the authority where this page is silent; the
vector files under `docs/vectors/` (generated by `server/cmd/vectors`, schemas
in `docs/VECTOR_SCHEMA.md`) are the executable form of every rule below. The
Go reference is `server/pkg/kilitcrypto`.

## 1. Primitives and encodings

| Primitive | Instantiation |
|---|---|
| DH | X25519; `Encode(PK)` = raw 32 bytes; every DH output is checked for all-zero (`lowOrderPoint`); every received X25519/Ed25519 public key must be exactly 32 bytes (`badKeyLength`) |
| Signatures | pure Ed25519 (RFC 8032); the 32-byte seed is the transport form of a private key |
| Hash / KDF | SHA-256, HKDF-SHA256 (RFC 5869), HMAC-SHA256 |
| AEAD | ChaCha20-Poly1305 (RFC 8439); wire form `ciphertext || tag(16)`; nonces are derived, never transmitted |

* All integers are big-endian: `u16BE`, `u32BE`, `u64BE`. `||` is concatenation. `zeros` = 32 zero bytes (equal to the empty HKDF salt).
* Usernames are canonicalised to lowercase ASCII `^[a-z0-9_]{3,32}$` before signing, AD construction or hashing.
* `device_id` is a lowercase UUIDv4 string (36 chars, `^[0-9a-f]{8}-[0-9a-f]{4}-4[0-9a-f]{3}-[89ab][0-9a-f]{3}-[0-9a-f]{12}$`).
* `server_origin` is the lowercase canonical origin `scheme://host[:port]` (no path, no trailing slash, default ports omitted).
* JSON: keys sorted, slashes unescaped. Receivers treat an absent optional key and an explicit `null` identically (`opk_id`, `att`, `ref`, `x3dh`, `opks_sig`, optional `created_at`).
* base64url (RFC 4648 §5) is unpadded. Decoders are strict: reject `=`, any character outside `A-Za-z0-9-_` (whitespace, `+`, `/` included), encoded lengths `≡ 1 (mod 4)` and non-zero trailing bits in the last character. A field that fails to decode is `bad_envelope` (client) / `400 bad_json` (server).

### Ed25519 strictness (unconditional, both platforms)

Before the library verify, every verifier checks:

1. signature length exactly 64;
2. `S = sig[32:64]` read little-endian is `< L`, where `L = 2^252 + 27742317777372353535851937790883648493`; as 32 LE bytes `L = edd3f55c1a631258d69cf7a2def9de1400000000000000000000000000000010`; compare `sig[32:64]` against these bytes from index 31 downwards, the first differing byte decides, equal rejects;
3. neither `A` (the public key) nor `R = sig[0:32]` is one of the 14 small-order encodings (libsodium's 7-entry `ed25519_blacklist` with both sign-bit values; the table is `ed25519_small_order_points` in `primitives.json`).

Violations → `badSignature`; a small-order public key at import time → `badPublicKey`; a public key that is not 32 bytes → `badKeyLength`. Ed25519 signatures are verified, never compared byte-for-byte across platforms (CryptoKit's are randomised).

## 2. Identity, prekeys and signed messages

| Key | Type | Lifetime |
|---|---|---|
| `IK_sig` | Ed25519 | permanent |
| `IK_dh` | X25519 | permanent |
| `SPK` (`spk_id` u32 monotonic, `created_at` unix s) | X25519 | rotated after 7 d; private key deleted when `now − created_at > 28 d` |
| `OPK_i` (`opk_id` u32) | X25519 | single use; private key deleted on first successful X3DH use or 7 d after its id first appears in a `consumed_ids` report, never on age; at most 500 stored |

Signed messages (all with `IK_sig`; `0x00` is a single separator byte; `nonce` is the 32-byte server challenge; `ts` is unix seconds corrected by the server offset):

```
ik_bind_sig    = Sig("Kilit_IKBind_v1" || IK_dh_pub)
spk.sig        = Sig("Kilit_SPK_v1" || u32BE(spk_id) || u64BE(created_at) || SPK_pub)
opks_sig       = Sig("Kilit_OPKBatch_v1" || u32BE(count) || (u32BE(opk_id_1) || OPK_pub_1) || … )   # batch in request order
register_proof = Sig("Kilit_Register_v1" || 0x00 || username || 0x00 || IK_dh_pub || 0x00 || device_id || 0x00 || nonce || 0x00 || u64BE(ts) || 0x00 || server_origin)
login_sig      = Sig("Kilit_Auth_v1"     || 0x00 || username || 0x00 || nonce || 0x00 || u64BE(ts) || 0x00 || device_id || 0x00 || server_origin)
delete_sig     = Sig("Kilit_Delete_v1"   || 0x00 || username || 0x00 || nonce || 0x00 || u64BE(ts) || 0x00 || server_origin)
```

Published bundle: `{username, ik_sig, ik_dh, ik_bind_sig, spk:{id, pub, sig, created_at}, opk:{id,pub}|null}`. The server verifies `ik_bind_sig`, `spk.sig`, `opks_sig` and `register_proof` on upload; clients re-verify `ik_bind_sig` and `spk.sig` on every bundle fetch and `ik_bind_sig` in every `prekey` envelope. An initiator rejects a bundle whose `spk.created_at` is older than 21 days (`staleSPK`). `identity_sign_pub` and `identity_dh_pub` are unique per server (`409 key_in_use`).

## 3. X3DH (Alice → Bob)

```
EK_A = fresh X25519 (the first draw from the key source; RatchetInitAlice draws the second)
DH1 = X25519(IK_dh_A, SPK_B)   DH2 = X25519(EK_A, IK_dh_B)
DH3 = X25519(EK_A, SPK_B)      DH4 = X25519(EK_A, OPK_B)        # only with an OPK
SK  = HKDF(ikm = 0xFF*32 || DH1 || DH2 || DH3 [|| DH4], salt = zeros, info = "Kilit_X3DH_v1_X25519_SHA256_ChaChaPoly", L = 32)
WS  = HKDF(ikm = SK, salt = zeros, info = "Kilit_WM_Secret_v1", L = 32)      # watermark secret
SID = SHA256("Kilit_SID_v1" || EK_A_pub)[0:8]                                # session routing hint
AD  = IK_sig_A || IK_dh_A || IK_sig_B || IK_dh_B || u16BE(len(uA)) || uA || u16BE(len(uB)) || uB   # initiator first
```

Bob mirrors the DHs with `(SPK_B, IK_dh_A)`, `(IK_dh_B, EK_A)`, `(SPK_B, EK_A)`, `(OPK_B, EK_A)`. Alice deletes the EK private key and the DH outputs, runs `RatchetInitAlice(SK, SPK_B)` and keeps `pendingX3DH = {ek_pub, spk_id, opk_id?}`, copied into every envelope of the session as the `x3dh` block (`kind:"prekey"`) until the first successful decrypt of a peer message on it; afterwards envelopes are `kind:"msg"`.

## 4. Double Ratchet (Signal §3, ChaChaPoly)

| Function | Definition |
|---|---|
| `KDF_RK(rk, dh)` | `HKDF(ikm = dh, salt = rk, info = "Kilit_DR_v1_RootChain", L = 64)` → `rk' = out[0:32]`, `ck = out[32:64]` |
| `KDF_CK(ck)` | `mk = HMAC-SHA256(ck, 0x01)`, `ck' = HMAC-SHA256(ck, 0x02)` |
| message keys | `HKDF(ikm = mk, salt = zeros, info = "Kilit_DR_v1_MessageKeys", L = 44)` → `key = out[0:32]`, `nonce = out[32:44]` |
| `ENCRYPT` | `ChaChaPoly.seal(pt, key, nonce, aad = CONCAT(AD, sid, header))`; wire = `ciphertext || tag(16)` |
| `HEADER` | 41 bytes: `0x01 || dh_pub(32) || u32BE(pn) || u32BE(n)`; `hdr[0] != 0x01` or any other length → `badHeader` before any KDF |
| `CONCAT(ad, sid, h)` | `u16BE(len(ad)) || ad || sid(8) || h` — the receiver uses the sid it recomputed from `ek` for `prekey` envelopes |
| limits | `MAX_SKIP = 1000` per chain (`Nr + MAX_SKIP < until` → `tooManySkipped`) |

`RatchetInitAlice`: `DHs = GENERATE_DH()`, `DHr = SPK_B`, `(RK, CKs) = KDF_RK(SK, DH(DHs, DHr))`. `RatchetInitBob`: `DHs = SPK keypair`, `DHr = None`, `RK = SK`. `RatchetEncrypt`/`RatchetDecrypt`/`SkipMessageKeys`/`DHRatchet` follow the Signal pseudocode verbatim; `GENERATE_DH` is the only consumer of the key source (`X3DH.initiate` draws `EK` first, then `RatchetInitAlice` draws the first `DHs`, then one key per `DHRatchet`; the responder draws nothing until its first decrypt, and an uncommitted candidate state never consumes a key). Decrypt runs on a copy of the state and commits only after the AEAD succeeds; encrypt persists the advanced state before the ciphertext leaves the device; a retry resends the cached envelope.

**MKSKIPPED eviction (exact):** entries `(dh_pub, n) → (mk, insertedAt)` are totally ordered by `(insertedAt, dh_pub bytes, n)`; all entries inserted by one `SkipMessageKeys` call share the `insertedAt` taken at call start. On every insert: (1) drop entries older than 7 days; (2) while more than 5 distinct `dh_pub` chains remain, drop every entry of the chain whose newest entry is smallest in that order; (3) while more than 2000 entries remain, drop the first entry in that order. An entry is deleted the moment it is used.

**Padding:** inner JSON → `json || 0x80 || 0x00*k` to the next multiple of 256 bytes (at least one byte added). Unpadding rejects a block that is empty or not a multiple of 256, all zero, or whose last non-zero byte is not `0x80` (`badPadding`). Inner JSON ≤ 40 960 bytes before padding (`sizeLimit`); a decoded envelope is at most 65 536 bytes (`413 envelope_too_large` on the server).

## 5. Envelopes

Server-visible body (`POST /v1/messages`): `{"to":"bob","msg_id":"<32 hex>","envelope":"<base64url(E2E bytes)>"}`. The server treats `envelope` as opaque and supplies `from` on delivery.

E2E envelope (UTF-8 JSON; `b64` = base64url):

```json
{"v":1,"kind":"prekey","sid":"b64(8)",
 "x3dh":{"ik_sig":"b64","ik_dh":"b64","ik_bind_sig":"b64","ek":"b64","spk_id":7,"opk_id":1203},
 "hdr":"b64(41 bytes)","ct":"b64(ciphertext||tag)"}
```

`kind` is `"prekey"` (with `x3dh`; `opk_id` may be `null` or absent) or `"msg"` (no `x3dh`). `sid` is present on every envelope (11 base64url characters). Inner plaintext JSON (`ttl` = conversation timer at send time, 0 = off, present on every message):

```json
{"t":"text","id":"uuid","ts":1760000000,"ttl":86400,"text":"merhaba"}
{"t":"image","id":"uuid","ts":1760000000,"ttl":86400,"att":{"blob":"32hex","key":"b64(32)","sha256":"b64(32)","mime":"image/jpeg","len":123456,"w":1200,"h":900}}
{"t":"ctrl.timer","id":"uuid","ts":…,"ttl":0,"timer":3600}
{"t":"ctrl.capture","id":"uuid","ts":…,"ttl":0,"seq_from":42,"seq_to":44,"count":3,"kind":"screenshot","ref":["uuid"]}
{"t":"ctrl.read","id":"uuid","ts":…,"ttl":0,"ref":["uuid"]}
{"t":"ctrl.delete","id":"uuid","ts":…,"ttl":0,"ref":["uuid"]}
{"t":"ctrl.resync","id":"uuid","ts":…,"ttl":0,"lost_sid":"b64(8)"}
```

### Control-message semantics

* **Dedupe** key is `(author, id)` per conversation (last 10 000 entries / 45 days); a duplicate is acked and dropped silently. `ref` lists resolve as `(expected author, id)`: `ctrl.delete` → the control's sender; `ctrl.read` → the receipt's recipient (the local user); `ctrl.capture` → either party (informational). An `id` that collides with a message of the other author is a different message.
* `ctrl.capture`: coalesced into one message per 2 s window per `kind` (`screenshot | recording_started | recording_stopped | blanking_unavailable`); `seq_from`, `seq_to`, `count` and `ref` are present for the first three kinds (each event consumes one per-conversation `seq`); for `blanking_unavailable` the counters are omitted or `null`, `ref` is `[]`, and `seq` does not advance. The receiver persists the highest `seq_to` per peer to render gaps.
* `ctrl.read`: optional, at most once per 5 s per conversation; only ids authored by the recipient of the receipt are honoured.
* `ctrl.delete`: only for an explicit user action on a message the local user authored; `ref` entries must name messages authored by the control's sender; the receiver deletes those messages, blobs and keys; other ids are ignored.
* `ctrl.resync`: the first inner message of a fresh session created by S3, by a bounded `unknown_sid ∈ known_sids` trigger, or by the rejected-initiation path. The receiver marks the session `lost_sid` superseded **even without `lastActivity`**, re-evaluates its sending session and re-sends on the new session its own messages sent on `lost_sid` within the last 7 days (oldest first, at most 50) with their original `id`/`ts`/`ttl`; each resend is a fresh ratchet encryption.
* Every control message is a full ratchet message (256-byte-padded, advances the sending chain); control messages are queued after text/image messages.

## 6. Attachments (v1, single shot)

`blob_id` = 16 client-generated random bytes (hex32 on the wire; `409 blob_exists` → new `blob_id` **and** new `fileKey`). `fileKey` = 32 random bytes drawn fresh for every seal (`att.key`).

```
HKDF(ikm = fileKey, salt = zeros, info = "Kilit_Attach_v1", L = 44) → key(32) || nonce(12)
AAD  = "Kilit_Attach_v1" || 0x01 || blob_id(16) || u16BE(len(mime)) || mime || u64BE(plaintext_len)
blob = "KATT" || 0x01 || ciphertext || tag(16)            # plaintext + 21 bytes
att.sha256 = SHA-256 over the entire blob file including the 5-byte header
```

The receiver must find exactly `att.len` bytes after `open`. Plaintext cap 8 MiB. Version `0x02` is reserved for the chunked format (`info = "Kilit_Attach_v2"`, `L = 36` → `key(32) || prefix(4)`, nonce `prefix || u64BE(i)`).

## 7. Safety number

Per party: `h = SHA256("Kilit_SafetyNumber_v1" || IK_sig || IK_dh || username)`, then 5199 times `h = SHA256(h || IK_sig || IK_dh)`; `h[0:30]` as six 5-byte big-endian integers, each `mod 100000`, zero-padded to 5 digits → 30 digits. Safety number = the two 30-digit strings, lexicographically smaller first (60 digits); shown in 12 groups of 5 and as QR `kilit-sn:v1:<60 digits>`. The keys are the identity keys bound in the session AD, which must equal the contact record.

## 8. Watermark payload

`tag = HKDF(ikm = WS, salt = zeros, info = "Kilit_WM_v1" || viewerUsername, L = 1)[0]`, `day = (unixSeconds / 86400) mod 16`, `crc4` over the 12 bits `tag || day` MSB-first with polynomial `x⁴ + x + 1` (init 0, no reflection, no final XOR); payload = `tag(8) || day(4) || crc(4)`. Tile 6×6 cells of 28 pt: the 20 border cells carry the sync sequence `10110100011100101001` (top row left→right, right column down, bottom row right→left, left column up), the inner 4×4 carries the 16 payload bits row-major, bit 15 at `(1,1)`. The `ws` used is the `wm_secrets` entry with the lexicographically smallest `sid`. `watermark.json` carries the exact integer delta map.

## 9. Local store (for completeness)

`storeKey = HKDF(ikm = masterKey, salt = zeros, info = "Kilit_LocalStore_v1", L = 32)`; per document `docKey = HKDF(ikm = storeKey, salt = zeros, info = "Kilit_LocalStoreDoc_v1" || relativePath, L = 32)`; `ChaChaPoly.seal(JSON, docKey, nonce = random(4) || u64BE(generation), AAD = "Kilit_LocalStore_v1" || relativePath || u64BE(generation))`. A document is accepted on load when its generation is `≥` the recorded value; a lower value or an AAD mismatch is `rollback` (deletes the session and triggers S3).

## 10. Inbound policy (§3.2b, identical on every platform)

For every fetched envelope:

0. **Outer dedupe before any KDF.** A `msg_id` already in `processed_msg_ids` (last 10 000 / 30 days) → ack silently (`already_processed`), no system row.
1. **Parse.** `v != 1`, unknown `kind`, `sid` missing / not strict base64url / not 8 bytes, `ct` shorter than 272 bytes, a `prekey` without a well-formed `x3dh` block → `bad_envelope`; `hdr` not exactly 41 bytes or `hdr[0] != 0x01` → `badHeader` (classified `bad_envelope`). For `prekey` envelopes `sid` is recomputed from `ek`; the carried field is ignored. A `dh_pub` that is not 32 bytes cannot occur in a 41-byte header, so `badKeyLength` is raised only by the ratchet API when called directly with a parsed header.
2. **Route.** `prekey`: verify `ik_bind_sig` and the 32-byte lengths (failure → `bad_envelope`). With a contact record, the carried `ik_sig`/`ik_dh` must be byte-equal, otherwise this is a **key change**: no session, no OPK consumed, the envelope is parked (`key_change`, 7 d) until the user accepts (S2). Without a record, `GET /v1/users/{from}`: byte-equal keys continue (trust on first use, the conversation is a pending *message request*); different keys or 404 → `identity_mismatch`; any transient failure parks the envelope (`lookup_deferred`, retries at 10 s, 1 m, 10 m, 1 h, then hourly; `lookup_failed` after 24 h). Then: if any session (live or superseded) has `sid == sid(ek)`, decrypt on it regardless of `seen_ek`; otherwise `ek ∈ seen_ek` (last 64, 45 d) → `replay`; otherwise a fresh initiation: `spk_id` not stored → `spk_gone`, `opk_id` not stored → `opk_gone`, else X3DH + `RatchetInitBob` on a candidate state, decrypt, and only on success commit the session, add `ek` to `seen_ek`, delete the OPK private key, apply S1 and re-run envelopes parked under this `sid`. An AEAD failure on the candidate is `decrypt_failed` and never triggers recovery. `msg`: select the session by `sid`; unknown `sid` → park (`unknown_sid`, 24 h); additionally, if that `sid` is in `known_sids` with `deletedReason ∈ {rollback, parse_failure}` → S3 (at most once per peer per 10 min). Then `RatchetDecrypt` on a copy of the state.
3. **On success, in order:** (a) append the plaintext and the outer `msg_id` to the conversation; (b) commit the ratchet state and `seen_ek`; (c) advance the cursor and append `msg_id` to `pendingAcks` in one write; (d) send the ack from `pendingAcks`, removing the id only on server confirmation. The server never redelivers a row with `id ≤ after`.
4. **Any other failure** is classified undecryptable with a reason (`bad_envelope, unknown_sid, identity_mismatch, lookup_failed, key_change_expired, parked_overflow, replay, opk_gone, spk_gone, too_many_skipped, decrypt_failed`), a system row is shown, and the envelope is acked. Never retried.
5. Every fetched envelope is acked exactly once, from `pendingAcks` (bounded 5 000), after it is stored, parked-then-resolved or classified; never while parked. Parked envelopes are bounded to 100 per peer (oldest acked as `parked_overflow`).

**Rejected initiation (recovery).** Triggers are exactly `spk_gone` and `opk_gone`, only for an accepted contact, and never when `sid(ek)` matches an existing session or is already in `known_sids`. The responder records `sid(ek)` in `known_sids` (`rejected_initiation`), classifies the envelope and immediately initiates a new session whose first inner message is `ctrl.resync{lost_sid: sid(ek)}`, at most once per peer per 10 min.

## 11. Sessions, supersession and the sending rule

Each session carries `sid`, `role`, `createdAt`, `lastActivity` (last successful decrypt of a peer message; own sends do not count) and a sticky `superseded` flag. Triggers:

* **S1 (peer moved on):** committing a new responder session supersedes every other session with that peer that already has `lastActivity`.
* **S2 (key change):** accepting a key change supersedes every session whose AD identity keys differ from the new record (`oldIdentity`) and resets `wm_secrets`.
* **S3 (local loss):** a session that fails the rollback check or cannot be parsed is deleted, its `sid` kept in `known_sids` (`rollback` / `parse_failure`), and a fresh session with `ctrl.resync{lost_sid}` is initiated at once. `known_sids` reasons `pruned` and `rejected_initiation` never trigger S3.

Superseded sessions still decrypt, never send, and are deleted 30 days after `lastActivity`; at most 4 sessions per peer (the oldest superseded is deleted, `pruned`). **Sending session:** among non-superseded sessions, if any has `lastActivity` the one with the lexicographically smallest `sid`; otherwise the newest own-initiated one; otherwise fetch a bundle and initiate. A crossed initiation therefore converges after one round trip without a clock.
